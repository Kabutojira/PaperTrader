# AI-native drug discovery to NVIDIA

## Decision

**Rejected — hypothetical positive beneficiary, low sensitivity and low confidence.** A concrete
Lilly program supports product relevance, but current evidence does not establish material,
repeatable life-sciences economics for NVIDIA common stock.

## Causal mechanism assessed

[[ideas/idea_ai_native_drug_discovery|AI-native discovery]] uses accelerated compute, models, data
infrastructure and automated wet labs. [[securities/security_33d9c44facc75c726c7d|NVIDIA]] can sell
compute and software into those workflows and can help integrate computational and laboratory
systems. The transmission becomes investable only when multiple customers create recurring revenue,
margin or cash flow material to NVIDIA's much larger Data Center business.

Sensitivity and confidence are **low**. The Lilly–NVIDIA program is an aggregate commitment of up to
USD 1 billion over five years for a joint lab, not a statement of NVIDIA revenue or profit. Pharma
can also internalize models and laboratories, and clinical translation rather than compute is the
idea's decisive economic bottleneck.

## Evidence

- Lilly's [co-innovation lab announcement](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nvidia-and-lilly-announce-co-innovation-ai-lab-reinvent-drug)
  describes up to USD 1 billion over five years and NVIDIA infrastructure and software. It does not
  allocate the commitment to NVIDIA revenue or establish recurring sector economics.
- The maintained idea says tool adoption is confirmed but a generalized improvement in clinical
  success is not, and warns that large pharma may internalize the workflow.
- NVIDIA's maintained review reports USD 75.246 billion of quarterly Data Center revenue; no
  life-sciences revenue, margin or cash-flow line is disclosed for comparison.

## Upgrade gates, contrary evidence and invalidation

Upgrade only after several pharmaceutical customers disclose repeat material NVIDIA platform
purchases, life-sciences revenue or software economics become measurable, or validated discovery and
lab productivity creates durable demand material to NVIDIA cash flow.

The hypothesis fails if pharma internalizes or selects competing infrastructure; AI workflows do not
improve discovery economics; the Lilly lab creates no repeat purchases; or NVIDIA continues to
disclose no material life-sciences economics. Rejection preserves the distinction between a useful
product example and an investable shareholder edge.

Review by **2026-11-15**, or sooner after material sector revenue disclosure, another large repeat
program or clinical evidence that changes customer demand.
