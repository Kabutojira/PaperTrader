This accepted edge identifies a direct path from data-centre power demand and grid constraints to
Eaton's electrical products, revenue, margins, and cash flow. Q1 2026 data-centre orders, electrical
backlog, segment sales, and margins confirm company-specific transmission. Acquisition leverage,
customer concentration, capacity execution, working capital, order normalization, and valuation
remain material limits, so the edge does not establish a current investment case.

## Causal mechanism

Higher-density compute requires switchgear, power distribution, power quality, thermal management,
and related electrical infrastructure inside data centres and across supporting grids. Eaton can
convert that demand through qualified Electrical Americas and Electrical Global products into
orders, backlog, revenue, segment profit, and cash flow. Q1 2026 gives direct evidence: Electrical
Americas data-centre orders rose about 240%, its sales rose 20% to USD 3.6 billion, and its operating
margin reached 25.6%; Electrical Global sales rose 21%, margin reached 19.2%, and backlog rose 73%.

Sensitivity is high because data-centre demand is already material to Eaton's electrical order and
revenue growth. Shareholder transmission remains conditional: hyperscaler concentration and project
delays can reverse orders, capacity expansion can pressure pricing and utilization, and the USD
11.079 billion of quarterly acquisitions raised debt and integration demands. Growth benefits the
common shares only if backlog converts into revenue and cash while margins and returns on invested
capital remain durable.

## Catalyst and confirmation

Strengthen the edge if Q2 and later primary evidence shows that data-centre and utility orders remain
broad, electrical backlog converts into revenue, margins and operating cash flow remain durable,
capacity additions earn acceptable returns, and acquisition integration reduces leverage without
raising working-capital intensity. Confirmation requires customer and project concentration to remain
controlled rather than order growth merely reflecting temporary over-ordering.

## Contrary evidence and invalidation

Downgrade or reject the edge if data-centre projects are cancelled or delayed, power efficiency or
architecture changes reduce Eaton content, customers internalize or dual-source equipment, new
capacity causes price or utilization deterioration, or electrical order growth fails to become
revenue and cash. The common-share relationship also weakens if acquisitions miss returns, leverage
constrains capital allocation, working capital remains elevated, or data-centre concentration drives
sustained margin compression.

Reconsider after the scheduled Q2 review and only if valuation satisfies the configured margin-of-
