# Defense and aerospace replenishment cycle

## Thesis

European rearmament, allied stockpile replenishment, and demand for air defence, missiles, sensors,
and engines can support a multi-year production and service cycle. Public budgets and current
backlogs confirm demand, but appropriations do not automatically become profitable revenue:
contract structure, capacity, suppliers, labor, export approvals, execution, and valuation decide
shareholder returns.

Current assessment: **the demand and backlog cycle is confirmed; security-level economics remain
unresolved**. RTX's current results show defense volume, margins, and cash improving, while its
commercial aerospace exposure, engine liabilities, contract risks, and valuation prevent a
theme-only strategy.

## Causal mechanism

1. Governments raise budgets and replenish depleted inventories while modernizing air, missile,
   naval, sensor, and engine capabilities.
2. Funded procurement becomes multi-year awards and supplier orders.
3. Primes and qualified suppliers expand production, reuse engineering, and grow aftermarket and
   sustainment revenue.
4. Earnings improve only when price, productivity, mix, and learning exceed labor, material,
   development, warranty, and fixed-price contract costs.
5. Ceasefires, political changes, procurement delay, capacity overbuild, or program failures can
   slow conversion and compress elevated expectations.

## Value-chain hypotheses

- **Missiles and air defense:** replenishment and new architecture can support long programs, but
  capacity and fixed-price learning curves determine margins.
- **Sensors, radars, and secure communications:** content grows with integrated systems, subject to
  export rules and rapid technical change.
- **Engines and aerospace suppliers:** military and commercial aftermarket can share capacity and
  technology, while certification, warranty, and fleet-remediation liabilities create offsets.
- **Munitions component suppliers:** sole-source positions can gain volume but may face government
  pricing pressure, qualification bottlenecks, and customer concentration.
- **Legacy platforms and cost-plus work:** can benefit from budgets yet lose share if procurement
  shifts toward cheaper, software-defined, or attritable systems.

## Confirmation gates

- budgets become appropriations, signed awards, funded backlog, and deliveries;
- production capacity and supplier throughput rise without quality failures;
- segment margins and cash conversion improve with volume;
- contract-loss provisions, receivables, and inventory remain controlled;
- replenishment demand persists beyond emergency orders; and
- valuation retains a margin of safety under normalized geopolitical and commercial assumptions.

## Catalysts

- funded multi-year missile, interceptor, radar, and engine awards;
- allied standardization and collaborative procurement that increases production scale;
- capacity additions reaching qualified output;
- service and aftermarket attachment improving recurring cash flow; and
- sustained segment margin expansion as backlog converts.

## Contrary evidence and invalidation

The idea weakens if budgets are delayed, reallocated, or fail to become contracts; ceasefires reduce
replenishment urgency; procurement reform fragments or cancels programs; suppliers cannot expand;
or fixed-price losses, inflation, and quality failures absorb volume. It is invalid as a security
thesis when commercial aerospace liabilities, customer concentration, cash conversion, or an
already-demanding valuation outweigh defense growth.
