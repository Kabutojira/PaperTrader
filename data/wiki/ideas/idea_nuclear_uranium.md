# Nuclear expansion and uranium fuel-cycle bottlenecks

## Thesis

Nuclear construction and life extension are increasing demand for uranium and fuel services, while
conversion and enrichment capacity are more concentrated and slower to expand than the mining-only
narrative implies. Contracted, operating suppliers can retain strategic value, but reactor growth
does not automatically translate into near-term equity returns because contract lags, operational
interruptions, project execution, and valuation determine cash realization.

Current assessment: **the fuel-cycle bottleneck is supported, but investability remains
security-specific**. The strongest evidence is the IEA's 2026 identification of tight conversion
capacity and concentrated mining, conversion, and enrichment, together with long-dated utility
contracting. A spot-uranium thesis alone is insufficient.

## Causal mechanism

1. New reactors, restarts, uprates, and life extensions increase multi-year fuel requirements.
2. Utilities secure uranium, conversion, enrichment, and fabrication years before fuel loading.
3. Concentrated conversion and enrichment capacity makes qualified supply and geographic
   diversification strategically valuable.
4. Suppliers with operating assets and disciplined market-linked contracts can convert scarcity
   into realized prices and cash over time.
5. Equity value depends on reliable production, contract structure, capital intensity, and the
   price already paid for long-duration scarcity.

## Value-chain hypotheses

- **Uranium miners:** operating, low-cost mines can benefit from contracting and higher realized
  prices; restarts, grades, jurisdiction, and capital needs can erase commodity upside.
- **Conversion and enrichment:** scarce qualified capacity may have stronger strategic leverage
  than mining, but regulation, customer qualification, state ownership, and expansion costs limit
  accessible public exposure.
- **Fuel services and reactor lifecycle:** fabrication, services, and maintenance can diversify
  commodity exposure, with contract execution and liabilities requiring separate analysis.
- **Utilities and reactor developers:** secure supply lowers outage risk, but high fuel prices,
  construction delays, and financing remain customer costs.

## Confirmation gates

- new contracting remains above replacement levels and contains economically useful pricing;
- mine, conversion, and enrichment expansions arrive on disclosed schedules and budgets;
- contracted volumes convert into realized prices, margins, and free cash flow;
- reactor construction, restart, and life-extension schedules remain funded; and
- security-specific valuation retains a margin of safety under normalized uranium prices.

## Contrary evidence and invalidation

The idea weakens if secondary inventories, underfeeding, recycling, mine restarts, or new conversion
and enrichment capacity restore ample supply; reactor projects slip or are cancelled; contract
prices lag costs; or operational outages prevent delivery. It is invalid as an equity thesis where
valuation assumes uninterrupted production and permanently acute scarcity.
