# Commercial space infrastructure and geospatial data

## Thesis

Government demand for proliferated, resilient space systems and commercial lunar services supports
a listed stack of launch, spacecraft, components, sensors, and data. Backlog and launch cadence are
necessary but insufficient: reliability, fixed-price execution, capital needs, customer
concentration, and recurring service revenue determine per-share value.

## Mechanism and value chain

Higher launch cadence lowers unit cost and supports constellation replenishment; common spacecraft,
components, and software can diversify mission revenue; data and connectivity can add recurring
economics. Launch providers, spacecraft suppliers, lunar logistics, Earth observation, and defence
sensors benefit only when backlog converts without failures, overruns, or dilutive financing.

## Confirmation gates

- launch and mission reliability improve with cadence;
- funded backlog converts on schedule and at positive gross margin;
- recurring space-systems or data revenue grows relative to bespoke projects;
- customer and program concentration declines; and
- operating cash flow funds development without repeated dilution.

## Invalidation

The thesis fails for a company after repeated mission failure, schedule slippage, fixed-price loss,
cancelled government programs, cash burn that forces dilution, or backlog that does not convert.
A possible SpaceX listing is not evidence and is excluded until a public filing exists.
