# Crude-product dislocation and refining margins

## Thesis

Crude supply can recover faster than refinery throughput and product exports, lowering feedstock
prices while gasoline and diesel remain tight. Complex refiners with reliable operations and useful
regional configurations can capture elevated cracks even when crude markets move toward surplus.
The opportunity is cyclical and operational: refinery restarts, weaker demand, outages at the
selected company, or regulatory costs can normalize earnings quickly.

Current assessment: **the crude-product dislocation is confirmed, but its duration is uncertain**.
The IEA observed four-year-high cracks in early July and Valero's first-quarter result showed strong
conversion to operating income and cash. The next evidence point is Valero's 30 July result; no
strategy is justified before current margin capture and valuation are reviewed.

## Causal mechanism

1. Crude production and tanker flows recover faster than damaged or constrained refinery capacity.
2. Crude prices and differentials soften while gasoline, diesel, and jet inventories remain tight.
3. Product cracks and gross refining margins widen for available, well-configured capacity.
4. Refiners convert the margin into cash only if utilization, feedstock access, turnarounds,
   compliance costs, and working capital remain controlled.
5. Restarts, product-demand weakness, new capacity, or crude re-tightening eventually close the
   dislocation.

## Value-chain hypotheses

- **Complex refiners:** can use feedstock flexibility and high utilization to capture regional
  cracks, but outages and turnaround schedules can dominate market conditions.
- **Simple or poorly located refiners:** may not access the advantageous crude slate or strongest
  product markets and can lag headline benchmarks.
- **Product consumers:** airlines, trucking, and industry bear the dislocation; demand destruction
  limits its duration.
- **Crude producers:** can face softer benchmarks even while downstream margins rise, so crude and
  refining exposure should not be conflated.
- **Biofuel and compliance markets:** renewable diesel, ethanol, RINs, and policy credits can add or
  subtract material earnings independently of petroleum cracks.

## Confirmation gates

- realized refining margin tracks relevant regional cracks after hedges and inventory effects;
- utilization and throughput remain high without deferred maintenance;
- operating cash flow exceeds sustaining, turnaround, and compliance capital;
- product inventories remain tight after seasonal adjustment;
- Middle East and Russian capacity returns more slowly than assumed; and
- valuation offers a margin of safety under normalized, not four-year-high, margins.

## Catalysts

- continued product-stock draws and slow export-refinery restarts;
- reliable operation through peak seasonal demand;
- discounted crude feedstocks and favorable differentials;
- disciplined cash returns after funding maintenance; and
- evidence that closures outweigh new capacity in relevant product markets.

## Contrary evidence and invalidation

The idea weakens if Middle East and Russian refinery throughput and exports normalize quickly;
gasoline, diesel, or jet demand disappoints; product stocks rebuild; crude differentials narrow
against refiners; new capacity ramps; or regulatory and renewable-fuel costs rise. It is invalid as
a security thesis if outages, turnarounds, working capital, debt, capital spending, or valuation
consume the apparent crack-spread benefit.
