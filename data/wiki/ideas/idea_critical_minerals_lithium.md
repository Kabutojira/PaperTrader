# Critical minerals and the lithium cycle

## Thesis

Lithium's 2025-to-2026 rebound reflects both a cyclical recovery from oversupply and stronger
battery demand, especially stationary storage. A late-decade shortfall remains plausible because
the downturn cut investment and projects require financing and commissioning time. The investable
claim is not that spot prices rise continuously: supply restarts, contract lags, chemistry changes,
recycling, and capital intensity can separate commodity prices from shareholder returns.

Current assessment: **cyclical recovery is confirmed; durable scarcity is plausible but
unconfirmed**. Operating producers show higher realized prices and volumes, while development-stage
projects still carry construction, funding, dilution, and schedule risks.

## Causal mechanism

1. EVs and stationary storage expand lithium-carbonate-equivalent demand, with LFP increasing the
   relevance of carbonate.
2. A low-price cycle cuts mine, conversion, and exploration investment and delays marginal supply.
3. Inventory depletion and disruptions can tighten near-term availability before contract prices
   fully reset.
4. Low-cost operating producers gain price leverage; funded projects gain option value if they
   reach production into a tighter market.
5. High prices invite Chinese restarts, new projects, recycling, sodium-ion, and material-efficiency
   responses, potentially restoring surplus before all announced capacity earns its cost of capital.

## Value-chain hypotheses

- **Brine and hard-rock producers:** benefit first from realized-price increases, subject to cost,
  grade, recovery, royalties, and contract lags.
- **Converters:** need reliable feedstock, qualified products, and utilization; raw-material price
  moves can compress rather than expand margins.
- **Development projects:** offer duration and policy optionality but transfer construction,
  financing, dilution, and commissioning risks to shareholders.
- **Battery and storage manufacturers:** benefit from lower lithium input costs and demand growth;
  they are not automatically positive lithium-price exposures.
- **Substitutes and recyclers:** sodium-ion, material efficiency, and recovered lithium cap the
  terminal scarcity case even if near-term penetration remains limited.

## Confirmation gates

- stationary-storage and EV demand stay strong after separating units from battery size and mix;
- realized prices rise with acceptable lags and operating margins rather than only spot benchmarks;
- inventories, receivables, and working capital convert into cash;
- project cancellations and capex cuts reduce future supply without impairing selected balance sheets;
- funded projects meet construction, commissioning, quality, and offtake milestones; and
- valuation remains supportable under mid-cycle rather than rebound pricing.

## Catalysts

- consecutive quarters of operating cash generation at producers;
- further storage deployment growth and contract repricing;
- disciplined project delays that tighten later supply;
- construction milestones and funding draws at high-quality development assets; and
- supply interruptions that do not simultaneously damage the selected producer.

## Contrary evidence and invalidation

The idea weakens if Chinese lepidolite or other idled supply restarts rapidly; new brine and
hard-rock projects finance and ramp on time; sodium-ion, recycling, or chemistry efficiency reduces
lithium intensity; EV and storage demand slows; or high prices restore surplus. It is invalid as a
security thesis when realized prices lag spot, working capital consumes cash, debt or capex forces
dilution, permits or commissioning slip, or the valuation already assumes sustained shortage.
