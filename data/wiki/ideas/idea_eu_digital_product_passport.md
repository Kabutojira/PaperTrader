# EU Digital Product Passport compliance infrastructure

## Thesis

The EU Digital Product Passport is moving from legislation into operating infrastructure. It can
create recurring demand for product identifiers, lifecycle data, registry integration, access
controls, and compliance workflows across regulated value chains. The regulatory need is real, but
open standards, a public registry, decentralized storage, and long implementation periods may
commoditize the stack or keep vendor revenue immaterial.

Current assessment: **the compliance buildout is confirmed; vendor economics are unproven**. The
first hard date is the battery-passport obligation on 18 February 2027, while most ESPR product
categories still depend on future delegated acts and transition periods.

## Causal mechanism

1. Product-specific rules require economic operators to assemble, maintain, and expose standardized
   product and lifecycle data through a unique identifier and data carrier.
2. Operators register DPP metadata centrally while hosting detailed data themselves or through a
   service provider.
3. Identifier, labeling, master-data, PLM/ERP, access-control, and integration vendors can sell
   implementation and ongoing connectivity.
4. Attractive returns require reusable cross-sector products, recurring revenue, and material
   attach rates rather than one-time consulting or commoditized QR generation.

## Value-chain hypotheses

- **Identifiers and data carriers:** RFID, QR, NFC, labels, printers, and scanners gain volumes, but
  standards and low-cost competition can compress margins.
- **Enterprise data and integration:** ERP, PLM, master-data, identity, and API vendors benefit where
  fragmented lifecycle data require trusted workflow integration.
- **Compliance services:** sector expertise can support implementation, though projects may remain
  labor-intensive rather than recurring software.
- **Manufacturers and importers:** face mandatory costs and data-quality risk; compliance does not
  automatically improve their margins.

## Confirmation gates

- delegated acts and access-right rules arrive on schedule with stable technical requirements;
- battery operators move from testing to production implementations before February 2027;
- vendors disclose material recurring DPP-related revenue or attach rates;
- integrations remain reusable across product groups; and
- security, uptime, and interoperability meet regulator and customer needs without costly rework.

## Contrary evidence and invalidation

The thesis weakens if delegated acts slip, transition periods defer budgets, data quality prevents
production rollout, or manufacturers build inexpensive internal tools. It is invalid as a vendor
theme if the public registry, open standards, generic integration, and low-cost identifiers
commoditize pricing or if compliance revenue remains immaterial relative to core businesses.

## Bounded research candidates

The repository does not contain verified immutable identities for the most direct identification,
labeling, and enterprise-data vendors. No ticker-only security operation, relationship, strategy,
signal, or order is created. Future intake should require an issuer to quantify DPP-related pipeline,
recurring revenue, and margin rather than relying on marketing participation.

## Confidence and review

Confidence is **high** in the legal and infrastructure rollout and **low-to-medium** in attractive
vendor economics. Review by **2026-11-30**, or sooner after battery access-right rules, service-
provider rules, production registry metrics, or a material vendor disclosure.
