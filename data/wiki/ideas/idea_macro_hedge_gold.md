# Gold and cash-generative miners as a macro hedge

## Thesis

Gold can diversify portfolios exposed to inflation, geopolitical shocks, and reserve-system risk,
while low-cost miners add operating leverage and distributable cash flow. The two exposures are not
equivalent: bullion avoids mine execution, tax, and cost risk, whereas miners can compound or destroy
the metal-price benefit. Record metal prices also raise mean-reversion and valuation risk.

Current assessment: **the hedge mechanism and current miner cash generation are confirmed, but a
new position is not yet supported**. Real yields, the US dollar, entry valuation, normalized costs,
and jurisdiction-specific liabilities require security-level work.

## Causal mechanism

1. Persistent inflation, policy uncertainty, and geopolitical stress increase demand for scarce,
   liquid reserve assets outside another party's liability.
2. Central-bank, ETF, bar, and coin demand can lift gold relative to currencies and bonds.
3. For miners, realized prices above all-in costs expand operating cash flow.
4. Shareholder value depends on reserve replacement, capital discipline, taxes, sustaining spend,
   and per-share returns rather than production growth alone.

## Value-chain hypotheses

- **Physical gold and backed vehicles:** provide cleaner metal beta but no operating cash flow and
  can suffer outflows when real yields or the dollar rise.
- **Low-cost diversified miners:** can translate prices into free cash flow and capital returns;
  reserve depletion, cost inflation, taxes, and project execution remain central.
- **Higher-cost or single-asset miners:** offer greater upside convexity but materially greater
  operating, financing, and jurisdiction risk.
- **Royalty and streaming companies:** reduce direct operating risk but can trade at premiums that
  remove the desired margin of safety.

## Confirmation gates

- real yields and the dollar do not overwhelm investment and central-bank demand;
- miners preserve all-in margins after sustaining capital, tax, and reclamation spend;
- reserve replacement does not require value-destructive acquisitions;
- capital returns improve per-share value through the cycle; and
- entry valuation remains supportable under a materially lower normalized gold price.

## Contrary evidence and invalidation

Faster disinflation, rising real yields, a stronger dollar, central-bank selling, ETF outflows, or
greater recycling can reverse gold demand. Miner exposure is further invalidated by cost escalation,
resource nationalism, operational failures, reserve depletion, or capital allocation that consumes
the windfall. A record spot price and one strong quarter are not durable valuation inputs.
