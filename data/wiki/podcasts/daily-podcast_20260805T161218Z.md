Welcome to the PaperTrader daily review for August 5, 2026. This is a paper-trading research program, not investment advice, not a brokerage service, and not a request to buy or sell anything. The research cutoff for this episode was 17:51 UTC. Everything discussed today comes from the frozen daily-cycle context, the completed decision snapshot, the canonical report, and the accepted research completed before that cutoff. No new research, no new prices, and no new conclusions are being introduced here.

The headline is straightforward: no trade, hold 100 percent cash. The reconciled model portfolio contains 10,000 euro of cash, zero gross exposure, zero pending orders, zero actionable signals, and no filled positions. That is a deliberate description of the current approved portfolio state, not a prediction about markets. The system also labels the cash stance provisional because valuation support remains incomplete across part of the broader coverage universe. Operational and investment-data health both require attention. Those conditions matter because an all-cash result can mean either that every opportunity was rejected on the merits or that the available evidence is not yet broad enough to responsibly deploy capital. Today it is the latter as well as the former for the reviewed names.

Two security reviews were accepted in this cycle: Rio Tinto and Kratos Defense and Security Solutions. Both involved current primary-source evidence and scenario-complete valuations. Neither produced a strategy, signal, order, fill, cash movement, position, or performance change. That common outcome is important. Strong operating results and interesting market moves are not, by themselves, sufficient grounds for a paper trade. The system requires a price-aware valuation, confidence, downside and payoff gates, and a current accepted relationship where applicable. If those gates do not clear, the model keeps cash rather than forcing activity.

Before turning to the individual companies, consider the market context. The snapshot records 51 research alerts, not 51 trading recommendations. A research alert is a bounded prompt to examine a price or technical transition. It may mark a Bollinger-band break, an RSI condition, a MACD crossover, or unusual volume. It does not settle the causal explanation, the valuation, or the portfolio action. Many alerts remain queued for high-priority security research. That backlog is one reason to keep a distinction between interesting movement and executable conviction.

The current assessed near misses make the same point in another way. Alphabet is rated Hold with a Watch action; its probability-weighted expected return is slightly negative and its base case is below the required entry threshold. Meta is also Hold and Watch, with a positive but insufficient expected return and several payoff and relationship gates unresolved. Microsoft has a positive base-case return and a double-digit probability-weighted expected return, but its bear-to-base and expected-to-bear payoff measures remain below the required thresholds, and it lacks a current accepted relationship in the deterministic gate. The point is not that these businesses are uninteresting. It is that a quality company can still be an unattractive deployment at a particular mark and under a particular risk framework.

The same discipline applies to sectors that are attracting attention. The research benchmark is separate from the approved model portfolio and cannot create signals, orders, or allocations. It is analytical only. The approved portfolio remains cash because no strategy has produced a current actionable signal and no candidate clears the full economic and evidence gate. Holding cash is therefore an active risk decision: it preserves optionality until a security has both supportable evidence and an entry with acceptable asymmetric payoff.

Let us begin with Rio Tinto. The accepted review updated the existing Rio Tinto research using the company’s 2026 half-year results. The operational evidence was constructive. Rio reported first-half revenue of 31.028 billion US dollars, underlying EBITDA of 14.826 billion dollars, underlying earnings of 6.851 billion dollars, operating cash flow of 9.173 billion dollars, and free cash flow of 3.834 billion dollars. Net debt was reported at 14.061 billion dollars. Within the portfolio of businesses, copper was a notable strength: copper underlying EBITDA rose 84 percent to 5.713 billion dollars. Iron ore remained the largest contributor at 6.769 billion dollars of underlying EBITDA, while aluminium and lithium contributed 3.311 billion dollars.

Those figures matter for the maintained critical-minerals and copper thesis. Rio is not a pure copper exposure; it is a diversified mining company with material exposure to iron ore, aluminium, lithium, and other operational variables. But stronger copper profitability and cash conversion add evidence that the diversified miner can benefit from a constructive copper environment. The review also resolved a prior unsupported-valuation gap. That is a genuine improvement in research completeness. It means the system moved from an honest inability to compare the security to a scenario-complete framework that can be audited.

Resolving a valuation gap does not automatically create a positive conclusion. At the frozen market reference, the immutable Rio identity was the sponsored ADR listed on the New York Stock Exchange in US dollars. Its August 4 close was 99.01 dollars, with a fresh US dollar to euro conversion rate recorded in deterministic state. The security was also in a persistent upper-Bollinger condition. That technical state can indicate strength, but it can also raise entry-risk questions after a move. The review did not treat the indicator as a reason to chase the price.

Instead, the research used bear, base, and bull fair values of 68 dollars, 93.50 dollars, and 126.50 dollars, with probabilities of 25, 50, and 25 percent. The resulting probability-weighted fair value was 95.375 dollars, below the 99.01 dollar reference mark. The deterministic assessment calculated a negative expected return of roughly 3.67 percent. The conclusion was Hold, with a Watch portfolio action, a Watch conviction tier, and allocation ineligibility. There was no paper strategy and no signal.

That conclusion should be understood narrowly. It does not say Rio’s results were weak. The first-half figures and copper contribution were evidence that improved the fundamental picture and allowed the prior valuation blocker to be resolved. It says that, using the maintained scenarios and the contemporaneous market reference, the expected value did not yet compensate for the required risks. The base case was below the current mark, and the deterministic gate retained exact ineligibility frontiers rather than substituting a vague judgment. Investors following the copper theme should therefore distinguish between a constructive commodity-related operating update and a sufficiently attractive entry point in the ADR.

The review did create one bounded follow-up: an idea-research refresh for the critical-minerals thesis. That follow-up is intentionally separate and sequential. The company review can inform the idea, but it does not rewrite the broader thesis in the same operation. This preserves the audit trail and prevents one company result from automatically expanding into a wider portfolio conclusion. For now, the Rio development is evidence for continued research, not a reason to deploy cash.

The second completed review concerned Kratos Defense and Security Solutions, ticker KTOS. Here the backdrop combined a positive operating update with a strong technical move. The immutable security identity is KTOS on Nasdaq, denominated in US dollars. The August 4 close was approximately 51.87 dollars. Deterministic market state recorded an upper-Bollinger breakout and a volume z-score of about 2.39. Those signals showed attention and a material move, but the research framed them as potential expectation and timing risk rather than as a buy trigger.

The underlying business update was substantial. Kratos reported second-quarter revenue of 458.8 million dollars and organic growth of 19.1 percent. Adjusted EBITDA was 38.2 million dollars. Consolidated backlog was 2.084 billion dollars, including 1.572 billion dollars of funded backlog. Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to a range of 1.750 billion to 1.810 billion dollars. These are supportive data points for the defense-technology demand thesis. They point to real customer demand, a large work pipeline, and improved top-line expectations rather than a price move without operating context.

The risks were equally concrete. The filed quarterly report showed 1.4376 billion dollars of cash and 139.9 million dollars of current and long-term finance-lease liabilities. It also recorded negative six-month operating cash flow of 38.4 million dollars. Share count had risen from 168,933,826 at year-end to 187,557,916 after a 16.4 million-share offering. Management’s full-year guidance called for only 30 million to 50 million dollars of operating cash flow while also guiding 125 million to 135 million dollars of capital spending. In other words, strong revenue and backlog did not eliminate the questions around free-cash-flow conversion, margins, capital intensity, and dilution.

The valuation made the tension visible. The accepted scenario values were 28.09 dollars in the bear case, 41.79 dollars in the base case, and 63.59 dollars in the bull case, weighted 25, 50, and 25 percent. The resulting probability-weighted fair value was 43.815 dollars. Relative to the 51.87 dollar market mark, deterministic calculation produced an expected return of approximately negative 15.53 percent. The security received a Sell rating, an Avoid portfolio action, a Watch conviction tier, and ineligible allocation status.

Again, this is not a dismissal of the defense theme or of Kratos’s reported growth. It is a reminder that a rising price can already incorporate substantial optimism. When a security is above the probability-weighted value, when free-cash-flow conversion is uncertain, when GAAP profitability is low, and when dilution has been material, the system does not promote a revenue and backlog story into a trade. The upper-Bollinger breakout and elevated volume may be confirmation of market enthusiasm, but they also increase the cost of being wrong about the next stage of execution.

There was no accepted idea-to-security relationship linked to Kratos in the canonical graph at this cutoff. That absence is itself part of the discipline. The system does not manufacture a relationship merely because a company appears related to a broad defense narrative. A causal link needs its own evidence, mechanism, direction, sensitivity, catalyst, and invalidation. Without that accepted relationship, and with the valuation gates failing, no idea follow-up and no conviction strategy were created. The correct output was research completion and a documented avoid decision, not a forced extension of scope.

Kratos had fast organic growth, backlog, and raised guidance but more pronounced valuation, cash-conversion, margin, and dilution concerns. One ended Hold and Watch; the other ended Sell and Avoid. The portfolio implications are therefore unchanged. Cash is 10,000 euro. Gross exposure is zero. Daily, cumulative, realized, and unrealized returns are all zero because the model has no positions. No pending paper order is waiting to fill. There is no hidden deployment implied by the research benchmark, no broker connection, and no live execution. Volume can confirm interest without establishing value. The appropriate response is to verify the period, inspect current primary evidence, compare the evidence with prior assumptions, and then let the deterministic valuation and risk gates determine whether a portfolio action is possible.

To close, today’s frozen cycle delivered two substantive research updates and no trade. Rio Tinto improved its research completeness through a supportable half-year valuation, but its probability-weighted value remained below the observed ADR mark. That restraint is the central message. PaperTrader is not designed to turn every headline, earnings result, or technical move into activity. It is designed to preserve uncertainty, retain the evidence trail, distinguish research alerts from trade signals, and wait until evidence, price, valuation, relationships, and risk controls align. Until they do, the current simulated decision is no trade: hold 100 percent cash.

Future cycles will revisit these conclusions only when fresh, verified evidence warrants another bounded review.
