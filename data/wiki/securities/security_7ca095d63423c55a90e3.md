## Current operating evidence

Rocket Lab's Q2 2026 issuer release reported record quarterly revenue of USD 234.066 million, up 62% year over year, and backlog of USD 2.36 billion. Product revenue was USD 181.347 million and service revenue was USD 52.719 million, demonstrating that the economics extend beyond launch services to spacecraft and components.

Gross profit was USD 84.576 million, but operating expenses of USD 142.090 million left a USD 57.514 million operating loss. The company reported a USD 49.258 million net loss for the quarter. For the first half, operating cash use was USD 134.407 million as working capital and continuing investment absorbed cash.

Liquidity was substantial at June 30: cash was USD 2.129 billion and current and non-current marketable securities totaled about USD 258 million. That liquidity came with material issuance. First-half ATM proceeds were USD 1.530 billion before USD 16.722 million of issuance costs, while common shares outstanding increased to 598.180 million from 543.575 million at year-end. Another 40.951 million preferred shares were outstanding, and Q3 guidance contemplated 641 million basic weighted-average shares including those preferred shares.

Management guided Q3 revenue to USD 250-265 million, but also guided GAAP gross margin to 29%-31% and adjusted EBITDA to a loss of USD 17-23 million. This combination supports continued growth while providing contrary evidence against assuming near-term GAAP profitability or uninterrupted margin expansion.

The same release says Rocket Lab closed the Mynaric and Motiv acquisitions and announced an agreement to acquire Iridium. It does not provide enough transaction detail to reverify the consideration, financing, assumed debt or final dilution used in the prior merger-aware valuation. Those prior figures are therefore not carried forward as verified facts.

## Business economics

Rocket Lab combines small-launch services with spacecraft, satellite components and mission systems. Revenue growth and a larger backlog can spread engineering and manufacturing costs over more programs, but the model remains exposed to launch reliability, qualification schedules, fixed-price execution, customer concentration, working-capital requirements and sustained research and development spending.

Neutron could expand the addressable launch market and strengthen the value of vertical integration. It is also a capital-intensive, pre-operational program whose schedule and economics remain assumptions. Management stated that first-flight Stage 1 tank production was aligned with delivery to the pad in Q4 2026; this is a milestone target, not proof of launch readiness or commercial returns.

## Downside, catalysts and invalidation

### Downside

- GAAP gross margin could remain around or below the guided Q3 range.
- Launch failures, mission delays or fixed-price overruns could reduce revenue and valuation multiples.
- Neutron may require more time and capital than assumed.
- Continued operating cash use or acquisitions could consume the reported liquidity.
- Equity issuance or transaction shares could push dilution beyond the scenario ranges.
- Iridium financing, leverage and integration could materially change per-share economics.

### Catalysts

- Q3 revenue at or above the USD 250-265 million guide.
- Margin recovery after the guided Q3 decline.
- Profitable conversion of the USD 2.36 billion backlog.
- Reliable Electron and HASTE execution.
- Delivery and qualification of Neutron hardware toward the stated schedule.
- Lower operating cash use and slower dilution.
- Complete, verifiable disclosure of Iridium financing and pro-forma share count.

### Invalidation conditions

The standalone valuation must be replaced if the Iridium acquisition closes, is terminated or receives definitive financing and dilution terms. It should also be reassessed if revenue misses guidance materially, backlog deteriorates, margin pressure persists, operating cash use accelerates, launch reliability weakens, Neutron slips materially or diluted shares exceed the modeled range without corresponding enterprise-value growth.
