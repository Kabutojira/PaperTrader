## Economics and thesis

Q1 2026 revenue grew 63% to USD 200.348 million and GAAP gross margin reached 38.2%. Space Systems
provided USD 136.685 million and Launch Services USD 63.663 million, so the exposure is broader than
one vehicle. Backlog was USD 2.220 billion, although the filing states that timing, termination,
modification, and fixed-price execution can prevent backlog from becoming profitable revenue.

The business still consumed USD 50.332 million of operating cash and lost USD 45.022 million. Cash
and marketable securities were USD 1.477 billion, but Q1 financing included USD 445.607 million of
net ATM proceeds. At quarter end, 575.768 million common shares and 45.951 million preferred shares
were outstanding; conversion and equity issuance therefore remain material per-share risks.
Neutron is still pre-first-flight and subject to qualification, schedule, reliability, and cost risk.

## Valuation, catalysts, and risks

The deterministic 2026-07-27 mark is USD 66.94, implying approximately USD 41.62 billion of equity
value on 621.719 million common-plus-preferred shares. A bounded 12-month revenue scenario uses the
filing's USD 1.424 billion of cash and marketable securities net of notes, borrowings, and finance
leases. The downside case assigns 8 times USD 0.9 billion revenue and yields USD 13.87 per share
(-79.3%); the base case assigns 15 times USD 1.1 billion revenue and yields USD 28.83 (-56.9%). These
are scenario values, not price targets: both multiples remain generous for a loss-making, cash-
consuming issuer and do not give backlog full face value.

Catalysts are reliable Electron cadence, backlog conversion at maintained margins, successful
Neutron qualification and first flight, and lower operating cash use. Invalidate if launch or mission
failures occur, Neutron slips or exceeds budget, fixed-price programs lose money, customer
concentration rises, acquisitions disappoint, or dilution outruns enterprise-value growth.
