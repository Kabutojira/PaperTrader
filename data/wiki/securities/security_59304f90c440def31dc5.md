## Business economics

RTX combines three businesses with different economic drivers. Collins Aerospace sells systems and services into commercial and defense markets. Pratt & Whitney sells engines and earns aftermarket revenue, while Raytheon supplies missiles, air defense, sensors and related systems. This mix diversifies demand but also exposes shareholders to commercial aviation cycles, engine-program economics, defense-contract execution and supply constraints.

The accessible issuer release reports Q2 2026 sales of USD 24.7 billion, 16% organic growth, adjusted EPS of USD 1.89 and company-defined free cash flow of USD 2.9 billion. Backlog was USD 289 billion, comprising USD 170 billion commercial and USD 119 billion defense. Collins sales rose 8%, Pratt & Whitney sales rose 16%, and Raytheon sales rose 18%. Adjusted operating margins were 16.7%, 8.3% and 12.6%, respectively.

Primary evidence: [RTX Reports Q2 2026 Results](https://raytheon.mediaroom.com/2026-07-23-RTX-Reports-Q2-2026-Results) (`source_web_de85bc376176672964a63825`).

## Thesis and contrary evidence

The positive case is that commercial aftermarket demand and funded defense programs convert the large backlog into profitable revenue and cash. The Q2 release provides evidence of broad sales growth and margin expansion, including higher Patriot, Standard Missile and AMRAAM volume at Raytheon and higher commercial aftermarket and military volume at Pratt & Whitney.

The contrary case is that backlog does not guarantee economic value. Commercial original-equipment mix, engine costs, supplier constraints, fixed-price defense work, restructuring and other excluded items can absorb volume benefits. The issuer states that forward non-GAAP measures exclude items whose variability and complexity can make reconciliation impracticable. Consequently, adjusted EPS and free-cash-flow guidance are useful valuation anchors but are not substitutes for GAAP statements or a current balance-sheet review.

## Downside

The completed downside case is USD 142 per share, materially below the supplied market close. It combines low-end adjusted EPS with a 20x multiple rather than assuming that current premium conditions persist. Downside mechanisms include adverse commercial-engine mix, higher remediation or restructuring costs, supplier shortages, weak fixed-price defense execution, slower commercial aviation demand, or backlog conversion that produces revenue without corresponding profit and cash.

The downside could be worse than the modeled case if adjusted guidance does not capture economically significant excluded items or if both earnings and the market multiple fall below the bear assumptions. Conversely, the model may overstate downside if cash conversion and margins remain stronger for longer than assumed.

## Catalysts and invalidation

Catalysts include delivery against the raised earnings and cash outlook, profitable conversion of the USD 289 billion backlog, continued aftermarket growth, additional defense volume, productivity improvements and sustained segment-margin expansion.

The thesis requires reassessment if RTX reduces adjusted EPS or cash-flow guidance, margins reverse materially, backlog fails to convert profitably, engine or defense-program costs accelerate, supply constraints restrict deliveries, or commercial and defense demand weaken. A persistent divergence between adjusted and GAAP performance would invalidate adjusted EPS as the primary valuation anchor.
