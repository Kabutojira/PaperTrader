# Microsoft Corporation common stock

## Research conclusion

Microsoft retains unusually strong operating momentum, but its valuation requires continued AI monetization and disciplined conversion of infrastructure spending into per-share earnings. Fiscal Q4 Azure and other cloud-services revenue grew 43%, Microsoft Cloud revenue grew 27%, and commercial remaining performance obligation reached USD 678 billion. Management subsequently guided to approximately 45% constant-currency Azure growth for fiscal Q1. These facts support a constructive earnings outlook, but more than USD 50 billion of expected fiscal Q1 capital expenditures and approximately USD 175 billion of calendar 2026 investment create substantial utilization, depreciation, and cash-conversion risk.

The updated 12-month valuation is **USD 418 bear, USD 567 base, and USD 698 bull**. Against the supplied 20 October adjusted close of USD 531.87, the base case provides moderate rather than compelling valuation support. Confidence is medium because demand and backlog are visible, while the eventual return on the infrastructure buildout is not.

## Business economics

Microsoft combines recurring productivity subscriptions, cloud infrastructure and platform consumption, enterprise applications, security, developer tools, advertising, Windows, devices, and gaming. Its principal economic advantage is distribution: established enterprise relationships allow the company to sell infrastructure, models, developer services, applications, and security through a common commercial channel.

Fiscal 2026 revenue was USD 331.839 billion, operating income was USD 155.237 billion, and operating cash flow was USD 182.935 billion. Adjusted diluted EPS excluding the reported OpenAI investment effect was USD 17.28, compared with USD 14.13 in fiscal 2025. Microsoft Cloud revenue reached USD 214.4 billion for the year, while Azure exceeded USD 100 billion and grew 41%. In fiscal Q4, Azure growth accelerated to 43%, Microsoft Cloud revenue rose 27% to USD 59.3 billion, and commercial remaining performance obligation increased 84% to USD 678 billion. These figures support durable demand, although remaining obligations do not determine future recognition timing or margin.

The portfolio is not uniformly strong. Fiscal Q4 Windows OEM and Devices revenue declined 7%, and XBOX content and services revenue declined 10%. The cloud and productivity franchises therefore carry most of the current growth thesis.

## Fiscal 2027 outlook and contrary evidence

Management expects fiscal 2027 revenue and operating income to grow at double-digit rates, operating expenses to rise in the mid- to high-single digits, and full-year operating margin to decline by less than one percentage point. For fiscal Q1, it guided to:

- Total revenue of USD 89.85–90.95 billion, representing 16%–17% growth.
- Intelligent Cloud revenue of USD 40.95–41.25 billion, representing 33%–34% growth.
- Approximately 45% constant-currency Azure growth.
- Productivity and Business Processes revenue of USD 36.7–37.0 billion.
- Relatively stable sequential Microsoft Cloud gross-margin percentage.

The most important contrary evidence is the scale of investment. Management expects fiscal Q1 capital expenditures above USD 50 billion and fiscal 2027 capital expenditures to grow year over year. It adjusted calendar 2026 capital-expenditure expectations to approximately USD 175 billion after changing the expected useful life of certain assets and shifting more future datacenter leases from finance to operating classification. This accounting change affects comparability: operating leases remain an economic commitment even when excluded from the stated capital-expenditure measure.

The thesis therefore depends not merely on demand growth but on utilization, pricing, energy availability, depreciation, and operating costs producing adequate incremental returns. Management expects fiscal 2027 to remain free-cash-flow positive, but that statement alone does not establish the level or per-share growth of free cash flow.

## Valuation

A forward earnings multiple is used because Microsoft is mature and profitable, while current free cash flow is unusually affected by infrastructure investment and lease classification. Fiscal 2026 adjusted diluted EPS of USD 17.28 is the verified anchor.

| Scenario | Probability | Normalized forward EPS | Multiple | Fair value | Principal drivers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | 25% | USD 19.00 | 22x | USD 418 | Azure slows, new capacity raises depreciation and operating costs, weaker PC and gaming trends persist, and the market requires a lower multiple. |
| Base | 50% | USD 21.00 | 27x | USD 567 | Azure and Microsoft 365 remain strong; Copilot and usage revenue expand; operating leverage broadly offsets higher infrastructure costs. |
| Bull | 25% | USD 22.50 | 31x | USD 698 | Azure remains unusually strong, capacity constraints ease, backlog converts efficiently, and AI throughput and custom silicon protect unit economics. |

The arithmetic is explicit: USD 19.00 × 22 equals USD 418; USD 21.00 × 27 equals USD 567; and USD 22.50 × 31 equals USD 697.50, rounded to USD 698. The base case receives a 50% probability because current demand and guidance are strong. Equal 25% tail probabilities reflect the unusually wide range of potential outcomes from AI capacity utilization, competitive pricing, and infrastructure economics.

## Thesis and assumptions

The constructive thesis rests on Microsoft's enterprise distribution, recurring Microsoft 365 revenue, Azure scale, developer ecosystem, security portfolio, and ability to offer customers multiple models on a common cloud platform. Management reported more than 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats and expects commercial-cloud growth to benefit from premium subscriptions and usage-based products.

The base valuation assumes normalized fiscal 2027 EPS of USD 21.00, continued double-digit Azure and Microsoft 365 growth, conversion of commercial obligations into profitable revenue, contained dilution, and no structural collapse in cloud margins. It does not assume that current Azure growth persists indefinitely.

## Downside and invalidation

Material risks include overbuilding AI capacity, lower model or cloud pricing, depreciation and power costs rising faster than revenue, operating-lease obligations obscuring economic investment, semiconductor or power shortages, cybersecurity incidents, regulation, and stronger competition from other hyperscalers and model providers.

The constructive case would be materially weakened if:

- Azure growth falls well below near-term guidance without a corresponding reduction in investment.
- Microsoft Cloud margins compress structurally rather than temporarily.
- AI infrastructure remains underutilized or commercial obligations convert slowly.
- Free-cash-flow conversion deteriorates over a full investment cycle.
- Normalized forward EPS cannot approach USD 21.00.
- Security, regulatory, partnership, or capital-allocation failures impair per-share economics.

## Catalysts

Relevant catalysts are delivery of approximately 45% fiscal Q1 Azure growth, profitable conversion of the USD 678 billion commercial obligation base, acceleration in Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud from Copilot and usage billing, stable cloud gross margin, improved AI throughput, and evidence that custom silicon or software optimization lowers serving costs.

## Confidence and limitations

Confidence is **medium**. The issuer's fiscal 2026 release and earnings-call transcript provide strong current evidence for demand and near-term guidance, but fiscal 2027 first-quarter actual results were not available in the opened evidence. The SEC-hosted annual-report and submissions URLs were not readable through the research broker in this operation, so the full Form 10-K was not independently re-examined and no assumption was made about the absence of later filings. Capital-expenditure comparability is reduced by the lease-classification change. Scenario EPS and multiples are analyst assumptions and remain highly sensitive to AI utilization, depreciation, margins, and durable growth.
