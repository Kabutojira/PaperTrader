## Business economics and thesis

SoFi combines consumer lending, deposit-funded banking, investing and payments products with enterprise financial technology. The economic thesis is that a regulated deposit base can lower funding costs while an integrated product set increases cross-buy and fee revenue. If growth produces durable earnings and book value per share without excessive credit losses or dilution, shareholder value can compound.

The contrary thesis is material. Unsecured personal lending introduces recession and underwriting risk; fair-value accounting can make reported results sensitive to model assumptions; deposit competition can narrow the funding advantage; regulation can increase required capital; equity compensation or issuance can dilute per-share progress; and weakness or concentration in the Technology Platform can reduce diversification.

These mechanisms remain analytically useful, but this review does not present the earlier operating statistics as verified current facts because the underlying primary documents could not be retrieved.

## Valuation method

Price-to-book remains the preferred framework for a regulated lender. A supportable 12-month valuation would use:

1. Current common equity and tangible common equity from a primary filing.
2. A reconciled diluted share count, including material dilution.
3. Current book value and tangible book value per share.
4. Scenario-specific per-share book-value growth driven by earnings, credit costs and issuance.
5. Multiples consistent with sustainable return on equity, growth, capital strength and risk.
6. Ordered bear, base and bull values with probabilities totaling 100%.

Those inputs were not independently available from the primary pages opened in this operation. Consequently, bear, base and bull fair values and probabilities are intentionally blank. The supplied USD 14.55 close on 2026-10-16 is a market reference, not enough to establish fair value without a verified book-value denominator.
