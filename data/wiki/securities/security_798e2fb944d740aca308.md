## Business economics

Arkema reports four operating segments: Adhesive Solutions, Advanced Materials, Coating Solutions and Primary Materials. Advanced Materials is the principal higher-margin earnings contributor, while coatings and primary-material activities add exposure to industrial demand, acrylic spreads, refrigerants and raw-material cycles. Adhesives provide another specialty-solutions earnings stream but were below their 2024 margin in 2025.

Consolidated 2025 sales were **€9.068bn**, EBITDA was **€1.251bn**, EBITDA margin was **13.8%**, adjusted net income was **€328m**, recurring cash flow was **€464m**, free cash flow was **€390m**, capital expenditure was **€636m**, and year-end net debt was **€3.170bn**. EBITDA had declined from €1.532bn in 2024, demonstrating the cyclicality and operating leverage in the portfolio.

Source: [Arkema key figures — source_web_2c1fb272054d9de0172d56af](https://www.arkema.com/global/en/investor-relations/financials/key-figures/).

## Current evidence and contrary indicators

Arkema reported Q2 2026 EBITDA of **€391m**, up **7.4%** year over year, with a **16.1%** EBITDA margin. Adhesive and Coating Solutions improved, and high-performance polymers gained momentum, but Performance Additives remained weak. Management described overall global demand as still weak, highlighted continued challenges in Europe, and reported approximately **€3.6bn** of net debt after the dividend.

Management retained guidance for 2026 EBITDA slightly above 2025 at constant exchange rates. It expected major projects to add approximately **€50m** of EBITDA versus 2025 and targeted capital expenditure of no more than **€600m**. These are constructive indicators, but foreign-exchange pressure, raw-material inflation, geopolitical disruption and weak end-market demand are material contrary evidence.

The issuer's results index showed Q3 2026 results scheduled for **November 5, 2026**, after this assessment date. Q2 is therefore the latest detailed earnings release used here.

## Valuation method

A 12-month **EV/EBITDA** framework is used because Arkema is capital intensive and materially leveraged. Depreciation, acquisition accounting and financing structure make a consolidated enterprise-value method more informative than applying an unadjusted multiple to current net income.

The share denominator is estimated at approximately **75.6m diluted shares**, inferred from €328m of 2025 adjusted net income divided by €4.34 of reported net income per share. Arkema publishes monthly share-capital statements, including a September 2026 statement, but the numeric detail is contained in a linked PDF and was not used to claim a more precise current count.

## Scenario drivers and probability rationale

### Bear — €30, 25%

The bear case assumes forward EBITDA falls to €1.20bn as weak global and European demand persists, raw-material pressure returns, and pricing fails to protect margins. Net debt rises to €3.75bn and the multiple contracts to 5.0x. A 25% probability recognizes meaningful cyclical and leverage risk without treating a renewed downturn as the central case.

### Base — €56, 50%

The base case assumes €1.30bn of forward EBITDA, modestly above 2025 and consistent with management's directional constant-FX guidance. Pricing, cost control and project contributions offset weak demand and currency headwinds, while net debt remains near Q2's €3.6bn. A 6.0x multiple reflects a mixed specialty/cyclical portfolio. This receives the highest probability because it most closely follows current guidance and Q2 evidence.

### Bull — €89, 25%

The bull case assumes €1.45bn of EBITDA, €3.40bn of net debt and a 7.0x multiple. It requires stronger Adhesive and Coating recovery, successful ramp-up of new capacity and acquired assets, and some demand normalization. The probability is limited to 25% because management had not yet reported broad demand recovery.

## Downside

The principal risks are prolonged industrial weakness, particularly in Europe; adverse currency translation; volatile raw-material costs and supply chains; weaker pricing discipline; project or acquisition-integration delays; and working-capital absorption. With net debt around €3.6bn at Q2, relatively small changes in enterprise value produce large changes in equity value. A return toward 2025 profitability or an increase in debt could move the shares toward the bear case.

## Catalysts

- Confirmation or improvement of full-year 2026 guidance.
- Continued year-over-year recovery in Adhesive and Coating Solutions.
- Delivery of the approximately €50m expected 2026 contribution from major projects.
- Successful ramp-up of PA11 and Rilsan Clear capacity in Singapore, PVDF expansion in the United States and other announced projects.
- Integration benefits from the acquired Dow adhesives activities.
- Lower working-capital requirements and visible deleveraging.

## Invalidation conditions

The base thesis should be reassessed if updated guidance implies EBITDA below €1.20bn, net debt remains above €3.75bn without corresponding earnings growth, project contributions are materially delayed, or pricing ceases to offset input-cost inflation. Sustained EBITDA above €1.45bn together with clear deleveraging would also invalidate the current range on the upside and require a new assessment.

## Confidence and limitations

**Confidence: medium.** The valuation is grounded in current issuer evidence, but forward EBITDA, net debt and multiples are analyst assumptions. Q3 2026 results were not yet available. No independently verified peer-multiple set or consensus forecast is incorporated. The diluted-share denominator is inferred rather than taken from the latest monthly statement. Arkema's key-figures page also presents some segment totals differently between its summary and historical tables, so this assessment relies on consolidated figures and does not construct valuation by summing segment data.
