## How the segments actually make money

### Space: launch + government/development

The Space segment recognizes external **Launch Services** and **Launch & Development** revenue, including commercial, government and Starship development work. In 9M2026, it reported **$2.108bn Launch Services** plus **$1.240bn Launch & Development**, for **$3.348bn**. Its nine-month operating result was **−$1.211bn**, reflecting significant development/R&D activity. Space should be valued for its reusable infrastructure and external contracted cash economics, **not** total launch count times a quoted list price.

The 10-Q records **110 Falcon launches in 9M2026**: **29 customer** and **81 internal**; also **three Starship launches**, all classified internal. Internal Starlink launches are **not intersegment sales**. Their associated deployment costs are economically part of the satellite network investment. Modeling all 110 Falcons as external revenue and then crediting a separate Starlink launch fee would double-count. Starship cost reductions and launch capacity are potentially valuable but must earn revenue or reduce costs before receiving DCF credit.

### Connectivity: Starlink's recurring economics

In 9M2026 Connectivity reported **$7.754bn consumer revenue** and **$5.042bn enterprise & government revenue** (including Starlink Mobile offerings): **$12.796bn total** and **$5.211bn operating income**. The company reported **13.6m consumer service lines** at September 2026 versus **7.2m** a year earlier; this metric **excludes managed enterprise/government contracts**. Q3 consumer ARPU fell to **$69/month** versus **$83** a year earlier; the 9M values are **$67** versus **$84**.

Key drivers: consumer subscriber growth versus international mix/ARPU compression, aviation and maritime penetration, terminal economics, satellite/network replacement intensity, spectrum licensing, service quality, and the pace of mobile adoption. Starlink's network integration with launch is a strong competitive advantage; adoption projections must not presume that every new international customer pays US residential prices.

Quick sensitivity: **10m incremental average paid consumer service lines × $65 monthly service ARPU × 12 = $7.8bn annual consumer service revenue** before churn, tax, hardware, price/mix changes and cost-to-serve. This is arithmetic, not customer guidance or consolidated incremental free cash flow.

### SI: compute, Grok, software and advertising

The 2026 10-Q now describes an **SI** segment (xAI/X and acquisitions). 9M2026 SI revenue totaled **$10.939bn**, made up of **$9.691bn SI Solutions & Infrastructure** plus **$1.248bn advertising**; it recorded a **−$3.295bn operating loss**, despite **+$0.431bn in Q3**, and **$39.995bn capex** in the nine months. SI's **1.7GW nameplate compute** at September 2026 versus **0.6GW** the year before is installed GPU-rated power, **not utilized, revenue-producing capacity**. The acquired Cursor business entered consolidated results in August 2026.

A credible SI model requires GPU/cluster commissioning, utilization, power availability, GPU refresh/replacement, customer contracts, net revenue retention, inference costs, model R&D, infrastructure finance and capital intensity. AI revenue should **never be set to zero** after 2025: that would ignore the majority of 2026 segment growth. Conversely, orbital AI data centers, transformative Terafab economics and hypothetical Tesla synergies are **speculative optionality**, excluded from the central cash-flow forecast unless supported by contracts and capex returns.

**How to reproduce the table:** each segment's annual revenue equals the preceding year's revenue × (1 + the case/period growth rate). The EBITDA proxy is the sum of segment revenue times an assumed segment EBITDA margin, **after treating stock-based compensation as an operating expense**. Base segment proxy EBITDA margins interpolate linearly from **Space −5% to 22%; Connectivity 60% to 63%; SI 22% to 40%**, between FY2027 and FY2036. Assumed D&A/revenue ratios interpolate from **Space 15% to 8%; Connectivity 20% to 15%; SI 50% to 23%**. Cash capex/revenue ratios interpolate from **Space 80% to 30%; Connectivity 34% to 23%; SI 160% to 25%**. All interpolations are annual linear percentages, not logarithmic changes.

Illustrative **EBIT = EBITDA proxy − D&A**; cash taxes = **20% of positive consolidated EBIT**, with no recognition of tax losses; incremental operating working-capital investment = **2% of the change in annual revenue**; **FCFF = EBIT − taxes + D&A − capex − incremental working capital**. EBITDA and FCFF are **model proxies**, not SEC disclosures. Financing, interest, exceptional investments, acquisitions, treasury-stock exercises, tax carryforwards, future equity dilution and potential orbital data-center spend are excluded. Share compensation is already treated as an expense and **not** added back a second time. No internal Space-to-Connectivity launch revenue is inserted.

**Major limitation:** the capex intensity declines dramatically by 2036 even though SI capex was **366% of SI revenue** in 9M2026. The base model still gives **negative FCFF in FY2027–2034**, with a peak deficit around FY2030 and a first illustrative positive year only in **FY2035**. If capacity growth needs remain high, the FCFF crossover could move much later. Negative FCFF is not automatically insolvency, but requires a funded cash/debt/equity plan. A serious valuation should explicitly model that plan before concluding on dilution.

## Price versus financial reality: reverse valuation

The latest completed Nasdaq close checked for this note is **$134.72 on 30 October 2026** ([dated SPCX history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spcx/history/)); the prior page's **$147.49** was the **23 October** close and must not be treated as current. This is **secondary market data**, not a PaperTrader canonical market-bar refresh.

Using the **13.579bn basic period-end Class A-plus-Class B shares** and $134.72 yields an **illustrative basic equity capitalization of $1829.36bn (~$1.83tn)**. Subtracting the simple net liquidity **$51.01bn** above implies an **illustrative EV of $1778.35bn (~$1.78tn)**, or **55.50×** the **$32.043bn** TTM revenue. This is **not** a class-specific fully diluted valuation or current live market cap: quote date and balance-sheet date differ; funding, claims, dilutive instruments and subsequent transactions remain unresolved.

**Reverse DCF sanity check only:** if all FY2027–2036 FCFF were zero, and one valued terminal cash flow at FY2036 with **3% terminal growth**, the illustrative enterprise value requires FCFF in **FY2037** of approximately **8%: $191.97bn; 10%: $322.88bn; 12%: $497.10bn** at respective WACCs. The **10% WACC** scenario implies about **$323bn of terminal annual FCFF**, an extraordinary burden of proof. This calculation ignores interim negative or positive cash flows and other claims; it is **not** a standalone fair value. **If interim FCFF is negative, the required terminal cash flow is even higher.** A 2036 DCF founded on arbitrary capex decay is not a sound basis for asserting that $100–150bn or any other point estimate is a defensible present enterprise value.

**Sensitivity checks worth re-running with audited 2026 figures:**
- Starlink service revenue: additional average consumer service lines × monthly ARPU × 12, then margins, reinvestment and taxes; do not treat the **13.6m end-quarter count** as a full-year average.
- Starship: customer launches and recognized contract milestones, not all internal flight tests or list-price extrapolation.
- SI compute: billable deployment/utilization × contracted revenue per unit, less power, GPU replacement, R&D, personnel and third-party margins; a **20% overrun on 9M2026 SI capex alone is ~$8.0bn**.
- Funding/dilution: share count, debt/refinancing and issuance costs at each annual cash deficit; keep controller voting dilution distinct from economic dilution.
- Discount rate: use at least 8%/10%/12% and terminal growth no higher than plausible long-run nominal expansion, taking care not to capitalize unsustainable above-economy growth.

## Correction and verification log versus the preliminary Deep Research

The preliminary Deep Research report supplies useful qualitative areas (reusability, Starlink, government contracts, Starship, AI capex) but its **financial model and recommendation are not suitable for direct adoption**:

1. Its FY2025 forecast table used **$19.7bn** total revenue despite citing **~$18.7bn**; the primary FY2025 consolidated figure is **$18.674bn**. FY2025 CFO less capex was **−$13.952bn**, not its **−$4bn** illustrative figure.
2. Its 2026 revenue forecast of **$20.9bn** was already **below the $27.083bn actually reported for 9M2026**. Its post-2025 zero-revenue AI assumption conflicts with **$10.939bn 9M2026 SI revenue**.
3. Its 2025/26 low-capex and subsequent rapid capex runoff are inconsistent with **$48.069bn 9M2026 capex**, of which SI alone **$39.995bn**. The enterprise-wide financing burden is decisive, not a footnote.
4. Its **$100–150bn enterprise-value** range and “reasonable versus private rounds” discussion are **not reconciled to the 2026 public capitalization (~$1.83tn)**, fully diluted common shares or a traceable cash-flow bridge. These claims are retained as **unvalidated prior research**, **not** accepted fair values or a Buy/Hold opinion.
5. Reported Starlink subscribers, enterprise/government sales, SI consolidation and GAAP segment margins give better investment anchors than broad undated industry projections or Wikipedia.

The research conclusion is **asymmetric**: SpaceX possesses durable launch and network advantages, demonstrated Connectivity profit and explosive SI revenues, but the *public equity* price embeds enormous monetization and reinvestment assumptions. Rapid growth is not evidence that cash flows will accrue to each Class A share at the required scale.

## Competitive landscape, risks, catalysts and invalidation

**Competitive strengths:** integrated Falcon/Starship engineering and launch capacity; installed Starlink network, terminals, operational know-how and large user base; recurring consumer and enterprise contracts; ability to coordinate satellite and ground-system iteration. External competitors include Amazon's LEO broadband initiative, Eutelsat OneWeb and terrestrial mobile/fiber networks for Connectivity, and commercial/government launcher alternatives for Space. SI competes against hyperscalers, GPU-cloud providers and frontier model/application platforms, whose capital costs and economics differ from satellite broadband.

**Key risks:** spectrum and export approvals; Starship cadence and failures; government fixed-price contract execution; launch concentration; customer price erosion; lower ARPU as international mix grows; regulatory and reputational risks around X; compute utilization and rapid GPU obsolescence; AI competition; data-center power/grid bottlenecks; commitments and refinancing; large stock-based compensation; acquisition integration; controlling shareholder and related-party conflicts; possible Tesla transaction; and recurrent share issuance. A valuation must separate already-contracted liabilities from discretionary optionality.

**Evidence-driven catalysts to monitor:** 2026 10-K and Q4 segment disclosure; Starlink consumer ARPU stabilization and enterprise margin; launches for paying customers versus internal deployments; third-party Starship contract conversions; verified SI installed/billable capacity utilization and customer/contract concentration; SI cash capex per incremental recurring revenue; cumulative FCFF and funding terms; new or amended credit facilities, stock issuance and option/RSU roll-forward; any definitive Tesla merger terms.

**Invalidate the favorable operating thesis** if Connectivity operating profit cannot fund replacement capex, SI growth relies on uneconomic price/contracting or perpetual hardware refresh, Starship costs do not decline despite scale, AI capital commitments accelerate faster than realized cash yield, or new financing materially reduces value per fully diluted share. Conversely, a supported valuation would require independent primary-data-based annual segment unit economics, a cash/debt/lease and issuance bridge, transparent post-split dilution and sensitivity-tested FCFF/terminal assumptions.
