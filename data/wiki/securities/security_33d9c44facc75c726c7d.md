## Business economics

NVIDIA sells an integrated accelerated-computing platform rather than a standalone chip alone. Its economics depend on combining accelerators, networking, systems, software, and developer tooling. Fiscal Q2 Data Center revenue represented the dominant reported business and grew faster than total revenue, showing that current earnings are heavily tied to the AI-infrastructure cycle.[^source_nvda_q2_fy2027_newsroom]

The reported **75.0% gross margin** indicates strong platform economics. However, the Q3 midpoint calls for a one-percentage-point sequential decline, and management explicitly excluded China Data Center compute revenue from guidance. Those facts are contrary evidence against extrapolating the recent growth and margin trajectory indefinitely.[^source_nvda_q2_fy2027_newsroom]

Vera Rubin was described as entering full production, while the company also reported continuing expansion across networking, agentic AI, enterprise software, automotive, robotics, and local AI.[^source_nvda_q2_fy2027_newsroom] These channels may broaden the platform, but most announcements do not disclose attributable revenue, margin, backlog, or customer economics. They therefore support the qualitative platform thesis rather than a separate incremental valuation.

## Thesis and assumptions

The thesis is that demand for training, inference, agentic AI, and physical AI continues to expand while NVIDIA retains system-level performance and software advantages. The latest reported quarter and Q3 guidance support near-term demand and earnings momentum. The valuation does not assume that present growth persists indefinitely.

The base case assumes:

- Q3 revenue guidance converts into reported revenue without a demand-driven miss.
- Vera Rubin production and customer deployment proceed without material disruption.
- Gross margin remains in the low-to-mid 70% range.
- CUDA, networking, systems, and software remain attached to accelerator demand.
- Export restrictions, custom silicon, customer concentration, and cyclicality cap the justified multiple.
- Forward normalized EPS reaches USD 11.50; this is an analytical assumption, not company guidance.

## Valuation

**Method:** twelve-month earnings multiple in the instrument's native currency, USD.

The starting evidence is non-GAAP diluted EPS of **USD 2.22** in fiscal Q2, 18% sequential revenue growth, 75.0% gross margin, and Q3 revenue guidance above the reported Q2 level.[^source_nvda_q2_fy2027_newsroom] Forward EPS is normalized rather than derived by mechanically annualizing GAAP EPS. Scenario multiples reflect growth durability, competitive position, cyclicality, export exposure, and execution risk.

The ordered scenarios total **100% probability**. The probability-weighted value is **USD 303.65 per share**. These are conditional fair-value estimates, not issuer guidance or externally sourced price targets.

### Bear case

Growth normalizes sharply as customers moderate infrastructure spending or obtain weaker-than-expected returns from AI workloads. Gross margin trends toward 70%, China remains unavailable for Data Center compute revenue, competing accelerators and open software reduce platform rents, and execution risk compresses the multiple to 22x.

### Base case

The current guidance converts, Vera Rubin extends the product cycle, and normalized earnings rise while gross margin remains structurally strong. The multiple remains below the bull case because customer-spending dependence, export controls, custom silicon, and rapid product transitions limit terminal certainty.

### Bull case

Training, inference, agentic applications, networking, and physical AI broaden simultaneously. Vera Rubin production scales cleanly, CUDA and networking attachment remain durable, and the market continues to capitalize the business as a high-growth platform rather than a cyclical semiconductor supplier.

## Downside and contrary evidence

The principal downside is that infrastructure spending can grow faster than customers' economic returns. If utilization, application revenue, or financing weakens, major buyers could slow purchases. NVIDIA also faces custom accelerators, alternative software, export restrictions, third-party manufacturing and memory constraints, product-transition risk, and a valuation that requires substantial future earnings.

The clearest verified contrary facts in the current evidence are that Q3 guidance assumes **no China Data Center compute revenue** and guides gross margin below Q2's reported level.[^source_nvda_q2_fy2027_newsroom] These do not negate the thesis, but they prevent treating recent growth and margin expansion as riskless or permanent.

## Catalysts

- Delivery against the USD 108.0 billion Q3 revenue outlook.
- Gross margin sustained near the guided range.
- Successful Vera Rubin production and deployment.
- Continued Data Center and networking expansion.
- Broader paid inference and enterprise adoption.
- Measurable economics from automotive, local AI, robotics, physical AI, and software.
- Evidence that demand is broadening beyond a small group of very large infrastructure buyers.

## Invalidation

The thesis should be invalidated or materially reduced if:

- Forward normalized EPS falls below the USD 9.00 bear path.
- Gross margin remains below 70%.
- Q3 guidance is missed because of demand weakness rather than timing.
- Vera Rubin deployment suffers a material delay.
- Export restrictions remove substantially more addressable demand.
- Major customers reduce AI capital spending materially.
- CUDA or networking attachment weakens enough that premium platform economics no longer apply.
