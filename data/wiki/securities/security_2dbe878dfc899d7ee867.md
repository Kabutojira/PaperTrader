## Current conclusion

## Business economics

Freeport operates large copper assets in the United States, South America, and Indonesia. Its earnings are driven principally by copper volumes, realized copper prices, mine and processing costs, and capital intensity. Gold and molybdenum by-product credits can materially reduce reported copper unit costs. These benefits are economically important but volatile, particularly while Indonesian production and downstream processing recover from the September 2025 Grasberg incident.

The [second-quarter 2026 results](https://s22.q4cdn.com/529358580/files/doc_news/2026/FCX_260723_2Q_2026_Earnings_Release.pdf) reported:

- USD 984 million of common net income and USD 0.68 diluted EPS;
- adjusted common net income of USD 1.1 billion and USD 0.74 per share;
- copper production of 786 million pounds and sales of 710 million pounds;
- realized copper of USD 6.17 per pound and consolidated unit net cash cost of USD 1.97 per pound;
- USD 2.048 billion of operating cash flow and USD 1.104 billion of capital expenditure;
- USD 4.1 billion of cash and USD 9.4 billion of consolidated debt at June 30; and
- approximately 1.4 billion common shares outstanding as of July 22.

The issuer expected 2026 operating cash flow near USD 8.3 billion under stated second-half assumptions, compared with approximately USD 4.3 billion of capital expenditure. That outlook is commodity-sensitive and included a USD 6.00-per-pound copper assumption. Q2 operating cash flow also included USD 0.7 billion of pre-tax insurance proceeds associated with the Grasberg incident.

## October operational evidence

The [October 2 third-quarter operational update](https://s22.q4cdn.com/529358580/files/doc_news/2026/FCX_261002.pdf) provides mixed but generally constructive evidence:

- consolidated copper production was approximately 830 million pounds and expected copper sales remained near the prior 750-million-pound estimate;
- gold production was approximately 230 thousand ounces, but about 60 thousand ounces of refined gold sales shifted from the third quarter to the fourth quarter;
- expected third-quarter unit net cash cost was approximately 5% above the prior USD 2.00-per-pound estimate, principally because deferred gold sales reduced by-product credits;
- expected realized copper exceeded USD 6.50 per pound;
- Grasberg mill throughput averaged approximately 140,000 metric tons per day, about 67% of normalized pre-incident rates;
- the Indonesian smelter restarted in late August and its ramp was proceeding in line with expectations; and
- management retained targets of approximately 80% Grasberg capacity by mid-2027 and near-full capacity by year-end 2027.

The update confirms operational progress but does not eliminate recovery risk. U.S. operations experienced flooding, power outages, and strong winds, while dry conditions in Indonesia created milling challenges. The figures were preliminary and subject to normal quarter-end procedures.

## Grasberg, ownership, and growth

At normal operating rates, the second-quarter release described PTFI’s underground operations as capable of approximately 1.7 billion pounds of copper and 1.3 million ounces of gold annually. Freeport owns 48.76% of PTFI while consolidating its financial statements, making minority interests essential when translating consolidated operating results into common-share value.

PTFI submitted an application to extend its Indonesian operating license. Under the disclosed memorandum, Freeport would retain 48.76% through 2041 and hold approximately 37% beginning in 2042. Completion of the formal license process and definitive arrangements remains important.

Growth options include leach initiatives, a potential Bagdad expansion, El Abra, and Kucing Liar. These assets provide long-duration optionality but require disciplined capital allocation. The Q2 release indicated that updated Bagdad capital estimates were approximately 30% above the earlier USD 3.5 billion estimate, while additional Kucing Liar investment through 2033 was estimated near USD 4 billion.

## Valuation method and arithmetic

A scenario price-to-earnings method is used because a single-point discounted cash-flow model would imply unsupported precision while commodity prices, Grasberg recovery, capital spending, and Indonesian economics remain unsettled.

- **Bear — USD 32.64, 30% probability.** Q2 reported EPS of USD 0.68 is annualized to USD 2.72 and valued at 12x. Copper and gold normalize, recovery or downstream processing slips, and restoration or project costs remain burdensome.
- **Base — USD 47.36, 50% probability.** Q2 adjusted EPS of USD 0.74 is annualized to USD 2.96 and valued at 16x. Grasberg reaches the stated milestones, but the forecast period still includes below-normal Indonesian production and material jurisdictional, minority-interest, and capital risks.
- **Bull — USD 65.12, 20% probability.** Annualized adjusted EPS of USD 2.96 is valued at 22x. Copper pricing remains favorable, Grasberg and the smelter ramp safely, deferred gold sales are recovered, leach gains continue, and Indonesian rights advance on acceptable terms.

The 30%/50%/20% distribution gives the greatest weight to the on-schedule but incomplete recovery. A substantial bear probability remains appropriate because production restoration, downstream processing, commodity prices, and government agreements are not controlled solely by Freeport. The bull probability is lowest because it requires favorable execution and commodity conditions simultaneously.

The multiples are assumptions rather than issuer guidance. The model deliberately does not capitalize the expected greater-than-USD 6.50 third-quarter realized copper price as a permanent earnings condition.

## Downside and contrary evidence

The principal downside mechanisms are:

- copper and gold price normalization from elevated realized levels;
- another safety event or delayed Grasberg recovery;
- continued sales timing or smelter bottlenecks;
- higher unit costs after reduced gold by-product credits;
- weather, power, water, or milling disruption;
- capital escalation at Bagdad or other projects;
- minority interests and lower post-2041 ownership limiting common-share participation; and
- adverse or delayed Indonesian license and ownership arrangements.

Q2 idle-facility and restoration costs were USD 284 million, and the first-half amount was USD 690 million. These costs were excluded from the issuer’s unit net cash-cost presentation but remain economically relevant.

## Catalysts and invalidation

Potential catalysts include safe completion of Grasberg material-handling upgrades, restart of Production Block 1S, achievement of the mid-2027 and year-end 2027 capacity milestones, realization of deferred gold sales, stable Indonesian smelter operations, additional leach production, a disciplined Bagdad decision, and acceptable definitive Indonesian operating-right agreements.

The positive operating thesis would be invalidated by another serious mine or safety event, missed recovery milestones, sustained sales below guidance, persistent processing bottlenecks, materially higher costs or restoration spending, weak cash conversion, uneconomic growth approvals, or adverse Indonesian terms.

The conservative valuation would require reassessment if Freeport demonstrates normalized earnings materially above USD 2.96 per share without depending on exceptionally high commodity prices, or if lower-risk attributable cash flow supports a sustainably higher multiple.

## Structural-copper relationship

The accepted relationship to [[ideas/idea_critical_minerals_copper|critical minerals and structural copper scarcity]] remains positive and high-confidence. Copper scarcity can raise Freeport’s realized prices, margins, cash flow, and reserve option value. The October update illustrates this sensitivity through an expected realized copper price above USD 6.50 per pound. Translation into common-share value still depends on attributable volumes, operating costs, capital requirements, minority interests, safety, and Indonesian terms.

## Limitations

Third-quarter earnings were scheduled for October 27, 2026 and were unavailable at the assessment time. The October update contained preliminary operating estimates rather than complete financial statements. The valuation annualizes one quarter of EPS and is therefore cycle-sensitive. A mine-by-mine discounted cash-flow model would require updated long-term production, commodity-price, tax, royalty, minority-distribution, and capital schedules that the opened evidence does not completely provide. Adjusted EPS also excludes costs that remain economically relevant.
