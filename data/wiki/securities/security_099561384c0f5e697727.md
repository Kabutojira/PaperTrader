# Canadian Solar Inc. common shares

## Decision

**Watch; no conviction strategy.** Canadian Solar combines global module manufacturing with a
fast-growing battery-storage business and a project-development platform, but current economics are
cyclical and capital intensive. First-quarter 2026 module shipments and revenue fell sharply year on
year, the reported margin depended materially on a one-time tariff refund, operating cash flow was
negative, and total debt rose. The July 31 bullish MACD crossover is a modest positive timing signal,
not evidence that these fundamental risks have cleared. At the USD 15.08 mark, the shares are just
above the USD 15.02 margin-of-safety buy zone and the base-case upside does not compensate adequately
for bear-case downside.

## Business and financial evidence

Canadian Solar operates through solar-module and system manufacturing, battery-energy-storage
solutions, and Recurrent Energy project development and ownership. Storage provides a credible
second growth engine, but module pricing, trade policy, input costs, project monetization and funding
conditions make consolidated earnings and cash flow volatile.

In Q1 2026, module shipments recognized as revenue were **2.5 GW**, down 42% sequentially and 64%
year on year, while storage shipments were **2.1 GWh**, up 142% year on year. Net revenue was **USD
1.078 billion**, down 10% year on year. Reported gross margin reached **25.1%**, but included a **USD
93 million tariff-refund benefit**; management guided Q2 gross margin to only 13–15%. Net loss
attributable to Canadian Solar was **USD 32 million**, or **USD 0.71 per share**, and operating cash
flow was negative **USD 209 million** as inventories increased.

At March 31, 2026, cash and cash equivalents were **USD 1.441 billion**, restricted cash was USD
421 million, and total debt including financing liabilities was **USD 6.8 billion**, up from USD 6.5
billion at year-end. About USD 2.3 billion was non-recourse Recurrent Energy debt, but manufacturing
borrowings, convertible notes and project-funding needs still expose common shareholders to
refinancing, dilution and asset-sale execution risk. Equity attributable to Canadian Solar
shareholders was **USD 2.830 billion**. The 2025 Form 20-F reported 67,816,724 common shares
outstanding at January 31, 2026.

Management guided Q2 revenue to USD 1.0–1.2 billion, module shipments to 3.1–3.3 GW and storage
shipments to 2.8–3.2 GWh. It reiterated 2026 U.S. shipment guidance of 6.5–7.0 GW of modules and
4.5–5.5 GWh of storage. These targets support storage growth and U.S. localization, but do not yet
demonstrate normalized profitability or positive free cash flow.

## Thesis, contrary evidence, catalysts, and invalidation

The constructive thesis is that a depressed module cycle obscures the value of Canadian Solar's
storage growth, U.S. manufacturing footprint and Recurrent Energy assets. Storage scale, project
monetization and localized supply could improve mix and cash generation if module discipline holds.

Contrary evidence is substantial: module volumes and revenue contracted, normalized gross margin is
well below the refund-assisted headline, inventory consumed cash, and debt is high. Solar
oversupply, silver and other input costs, tariffs, policy changes, customer concentration, project
approval and sale delays, foreign-exchange exposure, refinancing and convertible dilution can all
reduce per-share value. The ownership and financing complexity of operating subsidiaries also
limits how much consolidated asset value is economically available to common shareholders.

Catalysts are Q2 results that confirm storage shipment growth and 13–15% gross margin guidance,
working-capital release, project sales, lower manufacturing debt, profitable U.S. capacity and
sustained positive free cash flow. Invalidate or materially downgrade the thesis if storage growth
slows, normalized gross margin falls below guidance, module losses persist, inventories and debt
continue rising, project monetizations slip, liquidity tightens, or dilution materially increases.

## Valuation

The selected repository template is `other`, using a conservative `nav` method because Canadian
Solar combines cyclical manufacturing, non-recourse project assets, minority interests and complex
financing rather than fitting a pure compounder or commodity template. March 31 attributable book
equity of USD 2.830 billion divided by 67.817 million shares gives about **USD 41.73 book value per
share**. Large discounts are applied for debt, cash burn, minority interests, project execution,
cyclicality and uncertain realization of stated book value. The 12-month scenarios are:

- **Bear (30%): USD 10.43.** About 0.25x attributable book value as module oversupply, weak normalized
  margins, inventory cash use, refinancing and project-sale delays force a deeper asset-value
  discount. Return: **-30.8%**.
- **Base (50%): USD 18.78.** About 0.45x attributable book value as storage growth and project sales
  partly offset module weakness, debt and cash-flow volatility. Return: **+24.5%**.
- **Bull (20%): USD 27.12.** About 0.65x attributable book value as storage scales profitably,
  localized manufacturing improves mix, working capital releases and debt declines. Return:
  **+79.8%**.

The probability-weighted value is **USD 17.94**, for about **19.0% expected return** and **14.2%
confidence-adjusted expected return**. The configured 20% margin-of-safety buy-below price is **USD
15.02**. The current mark is slightly above that level, the base-upside-to-bear-downside payoff is
below the configured threshold, medium confidence limits the expected return, and no accepted
relationship exists. The assessment is therefore ineligible and does not justify conviction
strategy research.
