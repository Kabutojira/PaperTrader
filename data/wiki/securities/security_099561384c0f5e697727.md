# Canadian Solar Inc. common shares

## Decision

**Watch; no conviction strategy.** Second-quarter evidence confirms two opposing businesses inside
Canadian Solar: storage shipments grew strongly, while module volumes remained deeply below the
prior year and Recurrent Energy project sales slipped into the second half. Normalized gross margin
was only 13.9%, the attributable loss widened, operating cash flow stayed negative, inventories and
debt rose, and June attributable equity declined. The August 28 close of USD 13.14 is below the
updated USD 14.62 margin-of-safety buy zone and materially improves base-case upside, but the new
lower-Bollinger-band breach follows adverse fundamental evidence rather than a verified temporary
dislocation. Expected-to-bear payoff and the accepted-relationship gate remain insufficient, so the
research rating remains Buy / Initiate at watch-tier conviction without an actionable paper entry.

## Business and financial evidence

Canadian Solar operates through solar-module and system manufacturing, battery-energy-storage
solutions, and Recurrent Energy project development and ownership. Storage provides a credible
second growth engine, but module pricing, trade policy, input costs, project monetization and funding
conditions make consolidated earnings and cash flow volatile.

In Q2 2026, module shipments recognized as revenue were **3.1 GW**, up 25% sequentially but down
60% year on year. Storage shipments were **3.7 GWh**, up 82% sequentially and 73% year on year and
above guidance, although 471 MWh went to internal projects whose revenue is deferred. Net revenue
was **USD 1.2 billion**, up 12% sequentially and down 29% year on year. Gross margin normalized to
**13.9%** without Q1's tariff-refund benefit. Operating expenses rose to USD 240 million, and the
attributable net loss widened to **USD 77 million**, or **USD 1.40 per share**.

Operating cash flow was negative **USD 181 million** in Q2 and negative USD 389 million for the
first half as working capital consumed cash. June cash and cash equivalents were USD 1.461 billion,
with USD 389 million of restricted cash. Total debt including financing liabilities rose to **USD
7.1 billion**, including USD 4.1 billion at Recurrent Energy, USD 2.5 billion in Manufacturing and
USD 0.4 billion of convertible notes. Non-recourse Recurrent debt was USD 2.6 billion. Attributable
shareholders' equity declined to **USD 2.755 billion** from USD 2.808 billion at year-end, while
inventories rose to USD 1.656 billion from USD 1.134 billion.

Management expects Q3 revenue of USD 1.3–1.5 billion, gross margin of 13.5–15.5%, module shipments
of 3.5–3.8 GW and storage shipments of 3.4–3.8 GWh. It reiterated 2026 U.S. shipment guidance, but
said Jeffersonville ramp costs will weigh on profitability and that delayed Recurrent project sales
must close in the second half. This supports storage demand and U.S. localization while leaving
normalized profitability, working-capital release and project monetization unproven.

On August 13, Recurrent Energy disclosed USD 695 million of project financing and tax equity for
the fully permitted, under-construction 330 MW Cobalt Solar facility in California: approximately
USD 484 million of debt facilities and USD 211 million of tax equity. Commercial operation is
expected by the end of 2027. This reduces immediate asset-level funding uncertainty and supports the
project-monetization thesis, while construction, completion timing, project returns and economic
value attributable to Canadian Solar common shareholders remain unproven.

## Thesis, contrary evidence, catalysts, and invalidation

The constructive thesis is that a depressed module cycle obscures the value of Canadian Solar's
storage growth, U.S. manufacturing footprint and Recurrent Energy assets. Storage scale, project
monetization and localized supply could improve mix and cash generation if module discipline holds.

Contrary evidence is substantial: module volumes and revenue contracted, normalized gross margin is
well below the refund-assisted headline, inventory consumed cash, and debt is high. Solar
oversupply, silver and other input costs, tariffs, policy changes, customer concentration, project
approval and sale delays, foreign-exchange exposure, refinancing and convertible dilution can all
reduce per-share value. The ownership and financing complexity of operating subsidiaries also
limits how much consolidated asset value is economically available to common shareholders.

Catalysts are Q3 evidence that storage and module shipments convert at stable normalized margin,
working-capital release, completion of delayed Recurrent project sales, successful Cobalt
construction, lower manufacturing debt, profitable U.S. capacity and sustained positive free cash
flow. Invalidate or materially downgrade the thesis if storage growth slows, normalized gross
margin falls below guidance, module losses persist, inventories and debt continue rising, delayed
project monetizations do not close, liquidity tightens, or dilution materially increases.
