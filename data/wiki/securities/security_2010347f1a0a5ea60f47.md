## Business economics

Spotify operates Premium and Ad-Supported segments. Premium primarily earns recurring subscription revenue from users and distribution partners. Ad-Supported earns revenue from display, audio, and video advertising sold directly, through agencies, and through automated channels. Scale supports discovery, pricing, marketplace programs, and expansion into podcasts, audiobooks, video, ticketing, and AI-enabled products.

Content is the central economic dependency. Cost of revenue consists predominantly of royalties and distribution costs paid to labels, publishers, audiobook rights holders, and other providers. Royalty terms may depend on revenue, subscribers, churn, geography, and contractual thresholds. Spotify benefits when revenue and engagement grow faster than content and delivery costs, but concentrated third-party licensors retain substantial influence over a critical input.

## Current evidence

Spotify reported 777 million monthly active users and 300 million Premium subscribers at June 30, 2026, up 12% and 9% year over year. Q2 revenue was EUR 4.777 billion, up 14% as reported and 15% at constant currency. Gross margin reached 33.4%, operating income was EUR 655 million, and free cash flow was EUR 797 million. Trailing free cash flow was EUR 3.261 billion.[^1]

Premium revenue grew 15% as reported, supported by subscriber and ARPU gains. Ad-Supported revenue increased only 1% as reported, with advertising pricing softness partly offsetting impression growth. Operating expenses also reflected higher marketing and cloud/AI investment. MAUs missed guidance by one million, while Premium subscribers exceeded guidance by one million.[^1]

The June balance sheet showed EUR 5.938 billion of cash and EUR 3.450 billion of short-term investments. Exchangeable notes had been settled, and the filing reported no exchangeable-note balance. Q2 diluted weighted-average shares were 208.858469 million.[^2]

Liquidity is substantial but not entirely excess capital. At June 30, Spotify disclosed EUR 4.598 billion of contractual obligations and commercial commitments: EUR 2.290 billion of minimum guarantees, EUR 634 million of lease obligations, and EUR 1.674 billion of purchase obligations.[^2]

## Valuation

### Method

The method capitalizes normalized free cash flow, adds a conservative EUR 8.5 billion net-liquidity allowance, divides by 208.858469 million diluted shares, and converts euros to dollars at the ECB reference rate of USD 1.1193 per euro.[^2][^3]

The allowance is below reported liquid resources because leases, royalty guarantees, purchase commitments, working capital, and operating requirements reduce the amount that should be treated as distributable excess cash.

| Scenario | Normalized FCF | Multiple | Equity-value calculation | USD per share | Probability |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Bear | EUR 2.8bn | 18x | EUR 2.8bn × 18 + EUR 8.5bn = EUR 58.9bn | 316 | 25% |
| Base | EUR 3.6bn | 24x | EUR 3.6bn × 24 + EUR 8.5bn = EUR 94.9bn | 509 | 50% |
| Bull | EUR 4.3bn | 30x | EUR 4.3bn × 30 + EUR 8.5bn = EUR 137.5bn | 737 | 25% |

The probability-weighted value is:

`25% × 316 + 50% × 509 + 25% × 737 = USD 517.75 per share`.

### Scenario interpretation

- **Bear:** Subscriber or engagement growth slows, advertising remains weak, royalty and infrastructure costs pressure margins, and the market applies a lower cash-flow multiple.
- **Base:** Premium subscribers and pricing continue to grow, gross margin remains near the low-33% range, and reinvestment permits durable cash conversion.
- **Bull:** Advertising improves, broader formats monetize successfully, margins expand, and recurring growth retains a premium multiple.

The 25%/50%/25% distribution gives the base case the greatest weight while retaining broad tails because value depends on continued growth, durable margins, content economics, foreign exchange, and a premium multiple.

## Catalysts

The next fundamental test is delivery against Q3 guidance for 788 million MAUs, 305 million Premium subscribers, EUR 5.0 billion of revenue, 32.9% gross margin, and EUR 670 million of operating income.[^1]

Other potential catalysts include sustained Premium ARPU growth, improved advertising pricing, continued free-cash-flow growth, repurchases that reduce diluted shares, and measurable monetization of audiobooks, podcasts, video, discovery products, ticketing, and AI-enabled experiences. These remain potential drivers, not verified future outcomes.

## Downside and invalidation

The USD 316 bear value is approximately 46% below the supplied reference close. Downside could arise from weaker subscriber additions or retention, pricing resistance, adverse geographic or product mix, larger royalty obligations, ineffective growth investment, advertising weakness, currency movements, or multiple compression.

The constructive operating thesis should be reconsidered if:

1. paid-subscriber growth or retention deteriorates across multiple periods;
2. pricing ceases to offset product and geographic mix;
3. gross margin falls materially below the low-30% range without evidence of productive reinvestment;
4. normalized free cash flow persistently falls below EUR 2.8 billion;
5. content providers capture materially more of the platform's economics;
6. advertising weakness proves structural;
7. dilution persistently exceeds repurchases; or
8. reported liquidity is materially less available than assumed.
