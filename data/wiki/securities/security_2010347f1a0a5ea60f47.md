## Economics and thesis

Spotify combines a scaled subscription model with advertising and expanding music, podcast,
audiobook, video, and discovery products. Q2 2026 MAUs rose 12% year over year to 777 million and
Premium subscribers rose 9% to 300 million. Revenue was EUR 4.777 billion, up 14% as reported and
15% at constant currency; gross margin reached a record 33.4%, operating income was EUR 655 million,
and free cash flow was EUR 797 million. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow reached EUR 3.261
billion.

Liquidity is strong. Cash, restricted cash, and short-term investments were EUR 9.388 billion at
June 30. Spotify had repurchased 1.349 million shares for EUR 547 million in the first half, with
205.584 million issued shares net of treasury shares at quarter end. The thesis is continued
subscriber and engagement growth, improving monetization,
and durable margin conversion as the platform broadens beyond music. The counter-thesis is that
content economics, product reinvestment, advertising execution, competition from integrated
platforms, and equity compensation prevent the current free-cash-flow margin from compounding per
share at the valuation implied by the market.

## Valuation, catalysts, and risks

The mature-compounder/free-cash-flow-yield template uses the fresh USD 482.23 mark, current
USD/EUR rate of 0.86770, 205.584 million issued shares net of treasury shares, EUR 3.261 billion LTM
free cash flow and a conservative EUR 8.5 billion net-liquidity allowance. Those inputs imply about
EUR 86.0 billion of equity value and an enterprise-value-like 23.8 times LTM free cash flow.

The 12-month bear case uses EUR 2.8 billion normalized free cash flow at 18 times plus EUR 8.5
billion liquidity, yielding USD 330. The base uses EUR 3.6 billion at 24 times plus liquidity,
yielding USD 532. The bull uses EUR 4.3 billion at 30 times plus liquidity, yielding USD 771.
Probabilities of 25%/50%/25% produce a USD 541 weighted value. The cases respectively assume margin
normalization and weaker growth; continued low-double-digit growth with durable margins; or stronger
monetization and cash conversion. The output is sensitive to FX, working capital, content
commitments, equity compensation, repurchases and multiples, and is not a price forecast.

Catalysts are Q3 delivery, subscriber and MAU outperformance, sustained 33% gross margin, improving
advertising, and measurable monetization of discovery, audiobooks, video, AI products or live
experiences. Risks include concentrated third-party content licensors, price or churn pressure,
advertising softness, competition from Apple, Alphabet, and Amazon ecosystems, content and royalty
disputes, privacy and AI regulation, platform dependence, currency volatility, founder voting
control, and a valuation that already discounts continued cash-flow growth. Invalidate the thesis
if paid-user growth or retention weakens, gross margin reverses without productive reinvestment,
free cash flow persistently falls below the downside case, or content and platform economics erode
Spotify's ability to monetize engagement.
