## Economics and thesis

Spotify combines a scaled subscription model with advertising and expanding music, podcast,
audiobook, video, and discovery products. Q1 2026 MAUs rose 12% year over year to 761 million and
Premium subscribers rose 9% to 293 million. Revenue was EUR 4.533 billion, up 8% as reported and
14% at constant currency; gross margin reached 33.0%, operating income EUR 715 million, and free
cash flow EUR 824 million. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow reached EUR 3.2 billion.

Liquidity is strong. Cash, restricted cash, and short-term investments were EUR 8.8 billion at
March 31 after Spotify repaid EUR 1.304 billion of exchangeable notes and repurchased EUR 306
million of shares. The thesis is continued subscriber and engagement growth, improving monetization,
and durable margin conversion as the platform broadens beyond music. The counter-thesis is that
content economics, product reinvestment, advertising execution, competition from integrated
platforms, and equity compensation prevent the current free-cash-flow margin from compounding per
share at the valuation implied by the market.

## Valuation, catalysts, and risks

At the USD 511.25 close and the same-date USD/EUR rate of 0.87955, Spotify's Q1 diluted share count
of 209.28 million implies about EUR 94.1 billion of equity value. Subtracting a conservative EUR
8.0 billion net-liquidity allowance gives an enterprise-value-like figure of EUR 86.1 billion, or
26.9 times the EUR 3.2 billion trailing free cash flow.

The 12-month downside case uses EUR 3.0 billion normalized free cash flow, a 20 times multiple, and
EUR 8.0 billion net liquidity, yielding about USD 369 per share or 27.7% downside. The base case
uses EUR 3.5 billion free cash flow and a 26 times multiple with the same liquidity, yielding about
USD 538 or 5.2% upside. The base assumes continued growth and margins without expanding the current
cash-flow multiple; the downside stresses normalization and multiple compression. This bounded
range is supportable for comparison, but its base upside is below the configured 10% allocation
gate and far below the 20% margin-of-safety target. It is sensitive to foreign exchange, working
capital, equity compensation, share repurchases, content commitments, and the chosen cash-flow
multiple and is not a price forecast.

Catalysts are Q2 results, subscriber and MAU outperformance, sustained 33% gross margin, improving
advertising, and measurable monetization of conversational discovery, audiobooks, video, or live
experiences. Risks include concentrated third-party content licensors, price or churn pressure,
advertising softness, competition from Apple, Alphabet, and Amazon ecosystems, content and royalty
disputes, privacy and AI regulation, platform dependence, currency volatility, founder voting
control, and a valuation that already discounts continued cash-flow growth. Invalidate the thesis
if paid-user growth or retention weakens, gross margin reverses without productive reinvestment,
free cash flow persistently falls below the downside case, or content and platform economics erode
Spotify's ability to monetize engagement.
