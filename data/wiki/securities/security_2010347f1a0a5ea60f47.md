## Business economics

Spotify operates two reportable segments. Premium primarily earns subscription revenue from users and distribution partners. Ad-Supported primarily earns revenue from display, audio, and video advertising sold directly, through agencies, and through automated channels. The platform's scale supports pricing, discovery, marketplace programs, and expansion into podcasts, audiobooks, video, and AI-enabled products.

Content is the central economic dependency. Cost of revenue consists predominantly of royalties and distribution costs paid to record labels, publishers, audiobook rights holders, and other content providers. Royalty terms can depend on revenue, subscribers, churn, geography, and contractual thresholds. Spotify therefore benefits when revenue and engagement grow faster than content and delivery costs, but it does not fully control a major input to gross profit.

## Current evidence

Spotify reported 777 million monthly active users and 300 million Premium subscribers at June 30, 2026, up 12% and 9% year over year. Q2 revenue was EUR 4.777 billion, up 14% as reported and 15% at constant currency. Gross margin reached 33.4%, operating income was EUR 655 million, and free cash flow was EUR 797 million. Trailing free cash flow was EUR 3.261 billion.

The balance sheet showed EUR 5.938 billion of cash, EUR 3.450 billion of short-term investments, and no exchangeable notes at quarter end. Q2 diluted weighted-average shares were 208.858 million. These figures support substantial liquidity and cash generation, although the valuation reserves part of reported liquidity for leases and operating requirements.

The evidence is not uniformly favorable. MAUs missed company guidance by one million. Ad-Supported revenue increased only 1% as reported and 3% at constant currency. Operating expenses reflected increased marketing and cloud/AI investment. The filing also cautions that MAUs can overstate unique individuals because a person may maintain multiple accounts and non-bona-fide accounts may occasionally be included.

## Valuation

The method capitalizes normalized free cash flow, adds a conservative EUR 8.5 billion net-liquidity allowance, divides by 208.858 million diluted shares, and converts euros to dollars at the ECB reference rate of USD 1.1225 per euro.

The probability-weighted value is approximately USD 519 per share. That is effectively equal to the supplied USD 518.76 close and should not be interpreted as a precise price forecast. The primary sensitivities are normalized cash flow, the capitalization multiple, content costs, working capital, diluted shares, and EUR/USD translation.

### Scenario interpretation

- **Bear:** subscriber or engagement growth slows, advertising remains weak, royalty and cloud costs pressure margins, and the market applies a lower cash-flow multiple.
- **Base:** Premium subscribers and pricing continue to grow, gross margin remains near the low-33% range, and reinvestment permits durable cash conversion.
- **Bull:** advertising improves, broader formats monetize successfully, margins expand, and high-quality recurring growth retains a premium multiple.

The 25%/50%/25% probability distribution gives the base case the greatest weight while retaining substantial tails because a large portion of value depends on sustained growth and a premium multiple.

## Catalysts and downside

The next dated operating test is Q3 guidance: 788 million MAUs, 305 million Premium subscribers, EUR 5.0 billion of revenue, 32.9% gross margin, and EUR 670 million of operating income. Other potential catalysts include continued Premium ARPU growth, better advertising pricing, sustained cash generation, net share-count reduction, and monetization of audiobooks, podcasts, video, discovery, and AI-enabled products.

The bear value is about 39% below the supplied close. Downside could arise from weaker subscriber additions or retention, pricing resistance, adverse geographic or product mix, larger royalty obligations, ineffective growth investment, persistent advertising softness, currency movements, or valuation-multiple compression. Content licensors remain both necessary suppliers and major claimants on revenue.

## Invalidation conditions

The constructive thesis should be reconsidered if:

1. paid-subscriber growth or retention deteriorates across multiple periods;
2. pricing ceases to offset mix pressure;
3. gross margin falls materially below the low-30% range without evidence of productive reinvestment;
4. normalized free cash flow persistently falls below EUR 2.8 billion;
5. content providers capture materially more of the platform's economics;
6. dilution consistently exceeds repurchases; or
7. reported liquidity proves less economically available than assumed.

## Confidence and limitations

Confidence is **medium**. The opened operating evidence is issuer-authored and internally consistent, but it covers results only through June 30, 2026. Q3 results were not available in the opened sources. Free cash flow is non-IFRS and may vary with working capital. The EUR 8.5 billion liquidity allowance, future cash flows, multiples, and probabilities are analytical assumptions rather than company guidance. The supplied market close provides comparison context but is not used as a proxy for fair value.
