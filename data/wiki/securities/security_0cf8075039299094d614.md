## Business economics and thesis

Kratos develops defense hardware, software and systems across unmanned aircraft, propulsion, hypersonics and rocket systems, microwave electronics, radar, space and satellite ground systems, C5ISR, training and cybersecurity. Its strategy is to invest internal capital before full customer-funded production, seeking first-to-market positions in affordable systems that can later scale.

That model offers operating leverage if prototypes and early programs become funded production, but it shifts development, inventory, facility and dilution risk onto shareholders. Revenue growth and bookings are therefore necessary but insufficient: the central issue is whether they become durable GAAP operating income and free cash flow per diluted share.

The accessible [second-quarter financial release](https://www.kratosdefense.com/newsroom/kratos-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results) (`source_web_ec231a2e48f0ab6a225f7248`) reported Q2 revenue of USD 458.8 million, up 30.5%, including 19.1% organic growth. Government Solutions grew 36.4%, or 22.0% organically, while Unmanned Systems grew 8.1%. Quarterly book-to-bill was 1.1x, trailing-twelve-month book-to-bill was 1.3x, total backlog was USD 2.084 billion and funded backlog was USD 1.572 billion.

Demand is credible, but shareholder economics remain immature. Q2 operating loss was USD 1.6 million despite USD 38.2 million of adjusted EBITDA. For the first six months, operating income was USD 3.1 million, operating cash flow was negative USD 38.4 million, stock-based compensation was USD 31.3 million and acquisition spending was USD 346.8 million. The difference between adjusted results and GAAP cash economics remains material.

## Guidance, liquidity and capital intensity

Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to USD 1.750-1.810 billion and forecast organic growth of approximately 18%-23%. Full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance was USD 173-176 million, but operating-income guidance was only USD 16-22 million. Operating cash flow was forecast at USD 30-50 million against USD 125-135 million of capital expenditures, implying USD 85-105 million of free-cash-flow use.

Kratos also forecast USD 250-275 million of total 2026 investments spanning facilities, inventory, engines, unmanned systems and the Prometheus joint venture. These expenditures may create valuable production capacity, but they make near-term free cash flow a poor basis for a conventional discounted-cash-flow valuation.

Liquidity was substantial at 28 June 2026: cash was USD 1.438 billion, current assets were USD 2.357 billion, current liabilities were USD 425.3 million and finance-lease liabilities were USD 139.9 million. That strength was partly financed through shareholders: six-month proceeds from common-stock issuance, net of issuance costs, were USD 1.348 billion. Weighted-average diluted shares reached 190.1 million in Q2 versus 157.4 million one year earlier. Revenue growth must therefore outpace dilution to create per-share value.

## Evidence after the second quarter

On 5 October, Kratos announced [multiple space-and-cyber awards totaling more than USD 100 million](https://www.kratosdefense.com/newsroom/kratos-secures-more-than-100-million-in-contracts-for-space-and-cyber-mission-capabilities) (`source_web_0c68d656e895dd9c5abac7d8`). The work covers prototype mission applications, RF-signal processing and cyber capabilities using the KnownSpace sensor network. This supports continuing demand, but the release does not disclose contract duration, margins, revenue timing, customer identity or how much entered funded backlog.

On 14 October, Kratos reported the [groundbreaking for new AN/SPY-1 radar and Helios hypersonics facilities](https://www.kratosdefense.com/newsroom/kratos-breaks-ground-on-new-an-spy-1-radar-and-helios-hypersonics-testing-facilities-in-indiana) (`source_web_2a4d2e344b504bff75b43b9e`). The facilities are intended to expand radar sustainment, hypersonic-material testing and directed-energy capacity. The release references government funding and Kratos cost-sharing, but it does not provide a sufficiently clear allocation to model Kratos' exact cost or expected return. The project supports the production thesis while confirming continuing capital intensity.

## Downside, catalysts and invalidation

### Downside

- Funded backlog or recent awards may convert later, at lower margins, or not at all.
- Fixed-price execution, procurement delays, supply constraints and foreign-exchange pressure could prevent operating leverage.
- Unbilled receivables, inventory, facilities and acquisitions can continue absorbing cash.
- Stock compensation or additional financing could dilute per-share gains.
- A lower growth multiple would materially reduce value even if revenue continues rising.

### Catalysts

- Conversion of USD 1.572 billion of funded backlog.
- Revenue contribution from the October space-and-cyber awards.
- Successful jet-engine and Valkyrie production ramps.
- Productive deployment of radar and hypersonics facilities.
- Sustained book-to-bill above one.
- Clear improvement in GAAP operating margin and operating cash flow.

### Invalidation conditions

Reassess the thesis if organic growth falls materially below its high-teens trajectory, book-to-bill remains below one, funded backlog does not convert, key programs fail to progress into production, or adjusted EBITDA growth does not become stronger GAAP operating income and cash flow. The valuation is also invalidated if residual net cash falls below scenario ranges, diluted shares exceed modeled levels, or revenue multiples compress structurally.
