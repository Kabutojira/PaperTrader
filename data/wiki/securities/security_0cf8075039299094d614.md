# Kratos Defense & Security Solutions, Inc. common stock

Kratos is a smaller defense-technology supplier spanning unmanned systems, hypersonic and rocket
systems, propulsion, microwave electronics, space and satellite communications, training, and
cybersecurity. It is a thematic comparison for [[ideas/idea_defense_aerospace]] and
[[securities/security_59304f90c440def31dc5|RTX]], not an accepted canonical relationship.

## Economics and thesis

The investable thesis is that differentiated, internally developed systems can convert rising demand
for affordable autonomous aircraft, missiles, hypersonics, propulsion, and space communications into
sustained organic growth. The counterpoint is that Kratos is funding capacity and product development
well ahead of cash conversion, has low current operating margins, depends heavily on government
procurement and program execution, and already trades at a growth valuation.

Second-quarter revenue rose 30.5% to USD 458.8 million, including 19.1% organic growth. The Government
Solutions segment grew 36.4%, or 22.0% organically, while Unmanned Systems grew 8.1%. Consolidated
book-to-bill was 1.1x for the quarter and 1.3x for the trailing year; backlog reached USD 2.084 billion,
of which USD 1.572 billion was funded. These facts support demand and backlog conversion, but Q2
operating loss was USD 1.6 million and adjusted EBITDA was only USD 38.2 million, an 8.3% margin.

## Alert review, catalysts, and risks

From 7 July through 4 August the adjusted close rose 3.04% to USD 51.87. On 4 August it closed just
above the USD 51.8092 upper Bollinger band and volume registered a 2.39 standard-deviation anomaly.
The same date brought the Q2 filing and raised revenue guidance, providing current primary evidence
consistent with higher attention and stronger demand. The evidence does not establish that the filing
alone caused the move, and the breakout is not an opportunity at the assessed valuation; it is a
timing and expectation risk.

Catalysts are funded backlog conversion, Valkyrie and other unmanned awards, hypersonic and propulsion
production ramps, facility qualification, continued book-to-bill above one, and visible EBITDA and
cash-margin expansion. Risks are program delay or loss, fixed-price and supply-chain pressure,
government concentration, export and security restrictions, execution across recent acquisitions,
working-capital absorption, continued negative free cash flow, stock compensation, further dilution,
and valuation-multiple compression.

Invalidate the thesis if organic growth and funded backlog stop converting, major programs fail to
reach production, adjusted EBITDA growth does not improve GAAP margin and cash conversion, investment
needs persist without returns, or dilution prevents revenue growth from becoming per-share value.
