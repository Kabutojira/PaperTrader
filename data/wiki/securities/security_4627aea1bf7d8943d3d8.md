## Changes since the prior review

The scheduled Q2 evidence is now available and materially improves the operating picture. Sigma reported second-quarter production of 35.4 kt, sales of 24.4 kt and net sales revenue of US$54.7 million. Management also reported a 47% adjusted EBITDA margin, US$17 million of cash and US$125 million of net debt.

Those improvements do not close the valuation gap. As of the August 14 release, mining and plant operations had been temporarily paused since the week beginning July 17 while the company negotiated a terms-of-adjustment agreement with Minas Gerais authorities. Management was also evaluating financing alternatives to repay a US$95 million export prepayment balance. The opened evidence does not establish a subsequent restart, final regulatory terms, normalized cash generation, refinancing economics or a reliable diluted share denominator.

The prior unsupported conclusion is therefore retained, but its missing qualitative coverage has been repaired: the required assumptions, bear/base/bull conditions and explicit invalidation tests are now documented. No scenario value or probability has been invented.

## Business economics

Sigma owns its Grota do Cirilo operation in Minas Gerais through a wholly owned Brazilian subsidiary. Mine 1 supplies spodumene-bearing material to a dense-media-separation plant producing lithium oxide concentrate. Revenue is driven principally by tonnes sold, concentrate grade and realized lithium pricing. Economics depend on recovery and yield, mining and processing costs, freight, royalties, sustaining capital and working-capital conversion.

The [Q2 MD&A](https://ir.sigmalithiumcorp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Sigma-Lithium-2Q26-MDA.pdf.pdf) reports that production rose from 23.6 kt in Q1 to 35.4 kt in Q2, while sales rose from 23.6 kt to 24.4 kt and revenue increased from US$42.3 million to US$54.7 million. The [Q2 results release](https://sigmalithiumcorp.com/sigma-lithium-announces-record-2q-26-results-ebitda-margin-of-47-decrease-of-over-30-in-costs-tac-agreement-negotiations-underway/) attributes the improvement to higher output, a US$2,089-per-tonne realized price and lower costs, including reported AISC of US$668 per tonne.

This creates substantial operating leverage when production and prices are strong. It also creates downside leverage when operations stop: fixed obligations, debt service and expansion commitments can continue despite interrupted mine cash flow.

## Financial position and contrary evidence

Management reported a 47% adjusted EBITDA margin, the highest in the company's history, and said net debt fell to US$125 million. Cash was US$17 million at June 30. These are genuine improvements over the prior review, but adjusted EBITDA is an issuer-defined measure and does not demonstrate free-cash-flow conversion by itself.

The same release identifies material contrary evidence:

- Mining and plant operations were temporarily paused pending completion of a regulatory agreement.
- The company expected approximately US$1 million of procedural-adjustment capital expenditure and had agreed to pay up to approximately US$540,000 of fines.
- US$95 million remained outstanding under an export prepayment agreement, excluding US$11 million of collateral cash.
- Management was evaluating financing alternatives rather than reporting completed refinancing.
- Proposed output and expansion targets remained forward-looking and dependent on restart, ramp execution and capital availability.

The [Q2 financial statements](https://ir.sigmalithiumcorp.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Sigma-Lithium-2Q26-Financial-Statements.pdf.pdf) are explicitly unaudited and prepared by management on a going-concern basis. The accessible excerpt did not expose the detailed balance sheet, cash-flow statement, debt note, share-capital note and earnings-per-share note needed to independently complete per-share arithmetic.

## Valuation assessment

**Research status: valuation unsupported.**

A normalized enterprise-value-to-EBITDA approach is the most natural provisional method because mine output, realized concentrate prices and unit costs determine operating earnings. A supportable calculation would require:

1. normalized saleable production after regulatory restart;
2. scenario-specific realized lithium prices, grade and freight;
3. recurring AISC, royalties and maintenance capital;
4. a reconciliation from adjusted EBITDA to operating and free cash flow;
5. debt, export-prepayment and collateral balances after refinancing;
6. environmental and working-capital cash requirements;
7. expansion capital and its funding mix; and
8. verified basic and fully diluted shares.

The intended arithmetic is:

`normalized EBITDA × scenario EV/EBITDA multiple − net debt − other claims = equity value`

`equity value ÷ fully diluted shares = fair value per Nasdaq common share`

The evidence does not support the required inputs. The October 16 market close of US$8.53 is a market observation supplied in the research context, not a substitute for intrinsic value.

## Catalysts, downside and invalidation

Potential evidence-changing catalysts are completion of the regulatory agreement, full operational restart, sustained production and sales, positive operating cash conversion, collection of receivables, disclosed refinancing terms, net-debt reduction and fully funded expansion milestones.

Downside includes an extended suspension, lower throughput or recovery, weak lithium pricing, higher environmental spending, poor receivable conversion, expensive refinancing, liquidity pressure and dilution. Expansion can destroy value if committed before recurring cash generation and funding are secured.

The unsupported conclusion is invalidated only when primary evidence permits normalized free-cash-flow and per-share modeling: confirmed operations, sustainable volumes and costs, transparent cash conversion, complete financing terms, funded capital requirements and verified diluted shares. The operating thesis weakens if restart is delayed, regulatory costs rise, production deteriorates, refinancing fails or liquidity worsens.
