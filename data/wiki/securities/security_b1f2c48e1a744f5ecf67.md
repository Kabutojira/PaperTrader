## Economics and thesis

Labcorp combines a large Diagnostics Laboratories franchise with Biopharma Laboratory Services.
Q1 2026 revenue rose 5.8% to USD 3.538 billion, operating income rose to USD 380.8 million, and
diluted EPS rose 32.8% to USD 3.35. Diagnostics revenue grew 5.0% with 30 basis points of adjusted
margin expansion; Biopharma revenue grew 8.2%, including 11.3% Central Labs growth, with 60 basis
points of adjusted margin expansion. Biopharma backlog was USD 8.64 billion and book-to-bill was
1.04.

The thesis is that recurring diagnostic demand, health-system partnerships, specialty testing,
Central Labs scale, and automation support mid-single-digit revenue growth and operating leverage.
The counter-thesis is that reimbursement and payer pressure, labor and input costs, acquisition
integration, customer concentration, working-capital volatility, and a leveraged balance sheet
limit per-share compounding.

At March 31 Labcorp held USD 981.1 million of cash against USD 6.33 billion of debt, with the debt
increase partly reflecting pre-funding of maturities. Q1 operating cash flow was USD 191.5 million,
capital expenditure USD 121.0 million, and free cash flow USD 70.5 million. Full-year guidance was
raised to 5.0%-6.1% enterprise revenue growth, USD 17.70-18.35 adjusted EPS, and USD 1.24-1.36
billion free cash flow. The cash-generative franchise is solvent, but acquisition spending and net
debt warrant only a moderate balance-sheet score.

## Valuation, catalysts, and risks

The USD 314.23 mark is 17.4 times the midpoint of the issuer's USD 17.70-18.35 2026 adjusted-EPS
guidance. A bounded 12-month sensitivity uses the guidance range rather than inventing an earnings
forecast. The downside case applies 14 times the low end, giving USD 247.80 or 21.1% downside. The
base case applies 17 times the USD 18.025 midpoint, giving USD 306.43 or 2.5% downside. An upside
sensitivity of 20 times the high end gives USD 367.00 or 16.8% upside. These are comparison
scenarios, not price forecasts; adjusted EPS is issuer-defined and the chosen multiples are highly
sensitive to growth, reimbursement, leverage, acquisitions, and Q2 evidence.

Catalysts are the July 30 Q2 release, continued Diagnostics volume and price/mix growth, Central
Labs backlog conversion, health-system wins, specialty-test adoption, and sustained free-cash-flow
guidance. Risks include reimbursement and government-payer changes, wage and supply inflation,
billing or regulatory disputes, data security, customer and pharmaceutical-cycle exposure,
acquisition integration, working-capital swings, net debt, and multiple compression. Invalidate the
thesis if organic volume and price/mix weaken together, segment margins reverse without productive
investment, backlog conversion deteriorates, free cash flow misses the guided range persistently,
or leverage rises without commensurate earnings and cash generation.
