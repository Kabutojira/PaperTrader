# QUALCOMM Incorporated common stock

## Research conclusion

Qualcomm remains a profitable but transition-dependent semiconductor and licensing company. Automotive and IoT growth provides credible diversification, but fiscal Q3 2026 handset revenue contracted sharply and both major operating segments reported lower margins. A normalized earnings-multiple valuation produces 12-month bear, base, and bull values of **USD 102, USD 166, and USD 220**. The supplied October 22 market close of **USD 172.83** is modestly above the base estimate, so the current price already discounts some improvement beyond the base assumptions.

Confidence is **medium**. Current primary evidence supports scenario valuation, but the financial statements end June 28, while the supplied market price is from October 22. Full-year fiscal 2026 results were not available in the opened evidence.

## Business economics

Qualcomm has two principal earnings engines:

1. **QCT** supplies semiconductor platforms for handsets, automotive systems, and IoT applications. Its economics depend on design wins, product mix, leading-node supply, customer demand, input costs, and competitive performance.
2. **QTL** licenses Qualcomm's cellular patent portfolio. It has much higher margins than QCT, but depends on license renewals, device sales, enforceable intellectual property, and legal and regulatory outcomes.

In fiscal Q3 2026, consolidated revenue was **USD 9.947 billion**, down 4% year over year. GAAP diluted EPS was **USD 1.87**, down 23%, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was **USD 2.21**, down 20%. QCT revenue fell 5% to **USD 8.504 billion**, and its EBT margin declined from 30% to 26%. QTL revenue fell 3% to **USD 1.278 billion**, while its EBT margin declined from 71% to 69%.

The product mix was divergent. Handset revenue fell 20% to **USD 5.086 billion**, automotive revenue increased 61% to **USD 1.588 billion**, and IoT revenue increased 9% to **USD 1.830 billion**. Automotive and IoT therefore validate the diversification mechanism, but they did not yet offset handset weakness and margin compression.

Management guided fiscal Q4 revenue to **USD 9.7-10.5 billion** and non-GAAP diluted EPS to **USD 2.05-2.25**. It also reported broad increases in wafer fabrication, assembly, test, advanced packaging, memory, and other material costs, with pricing expected to help margins gradually rather than immediately.

## Financial position and cash economics

At June 28, Qualcomm held **USD 8.304 billion** of cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities against **USD 15.270 billion** of debt. Nine-month operating cash flow was **USD 8.405 billion**, compared with USD 10.016 billion in the prior-year period, and capital expenditure was **USD 1.578 billion**. The company spent USD 6.806 billion on repurchases and USD 2.868 billion on dividends during the nine months.

Inventory rose to **USD 8.379 billion** from USD 6.526 billion at fiscal year-end. Management connected the increase partly to customer-demand effects from memory supply constraints. This working-capital build, net debt, acquisition spending, and substantial capital returns reduce balance-sheet protection even though management reported adequate expected liquidity.

Nine-month GAAP net income is not a clean valuation base. It includes a large income-tax benefit and investment gains, while non-GAAP earnings exclude share-based compensation and acquisition-related items. The valuation therefore anchors to current quarterly non-GAAP earning power but applies explicit normalization and conservative scenario ranges.

## Thesis and contrary evidence

### Constructive thesis

Qualcomm can use its modem, connectivity, low-power computing, edge-AI, and patent-licensing capabilities to extend earnings beyond mobile handsets. Automotive and IoT growth provides current evidence that this expansion is real, while QTL remains a high-margin source of cash generation. PCs and data-center products add potential upside if they become meaningful contributors rather than product announcements alone.

### Contrary evidence

The transition is not yet complete. Handset revenue fell faster than automotive and IoT grew in aggregate dollars during fiscal Q3, and both QCT and QTL margins contracted. Qualcomm also expects Apple increasingly to use internally developed modems, which management says will have a significant negative effect on QCT revenue, operating results, and cash flow.

Other material risks include concentrated customers and licensees, Samsung and other customer insourcing, China exposure, trade restrictions, sole- or limited-source suppliers, higher leading-node and memory costs, excess inventory, licensing disputes, competition, acquisitions, legal proceedings, debt, and dilution from share-based compensation.

## Valuation

### Method

A 12-month normalized earnings multiple is used because Qualcomm has established profitability and observable per-share earnings. Fiscal Q3 non-GAAP EPS of USD 2.21 and fiscal Q4 guidance of USD 2.05-2.25 imply an approximately USD 8.2-9.0 annualized near-term range. Scenario EPS extends below and above that reference according to the operating evidence.

The method does not capitalize the unusual fiscal 2026 tax benefit or investment gains. It also does not accept management's fiscal 2027 and 2029 non-handset objectives as already earned.

### Scenarios

- **Bear — 30% probability: USD 102.** Normalized forward EPS of USD 8.50 multiplied by 12x. Persistent handset erosion, faster Apple insourcing, input-cost pressure, excess inventory, and weaker QCT margins outweigh diversification.
- **Base — 50% probability: USD 166.** Normalized forward EPS of USD 9.75 multiplied by 17x equals USD 165.75, rounded to USD 166. Handsets become less adverse, pricing gradually offsets cost inflation, QTL remains durable, and automotive and IoT continue expanding.
- **Bull — 20% probability: USD 220.** Normalized forward EPS of USD 11.00 multiplied by 20x. Automotive and IoT maintain strong growth, PCs and data-center products become material, pricing restores margins, and licensing remains stable despite customer insourcing.

The probabilities total 100%. The bear probability is meaningful because current handset contraction and Apple insourcing are evidenced rather than hypothetical. The bull probability is lowest because much of the expected non-handset acceleration remains forward-looking.

## Downside, catalysts, and invalidation

**Downside:** Continued handset contraction, customer silicon insourcing, lower QCT or QTL margins, failed pricing, excess inventory, China restrictions, supply disruption, weak licensing renewals, adverse legal outcomes, acquisition costs, rising leverage, or dilution could push normalized EPS and the appropriate multiple toward the bear case.

**Catalysts:** Handset stabilization, evidence that pricing is restoring QCT margins, sustained automotive and IoT expansion, material PC or data-center revenue, strong licensing renewals, lower inventory, improved cash conversion, and genuinely accretive repurchases could support the bull case.

**Invalidation:** Materially weaken the constructive thesis if non-handset profit fails to offset handset and Apple-related losses, QCT or QTL margins keep contracting, licensing economics deteriorate, inventory requires significant charges, or cash generation weakens while debt or dilution rises. Weaken the bear thesis if reported results demonstrate sustained non-handset earnings acceleration and durable margin recovery.

## Confidence and limitations

Confidence is **medium**. The valuation is grounded in current issuer financial evidence, but scenario EPS, multiples, and probabilities remain analytical assumptions. The newest opened financial period ended June 28, 2026, while the supplied market price is dated October 22. Non-GAAP earnings exclude substantial share-based compensation and acquisition items, and the exact future profit contribution from automotive, IoT, PCs, and data centers is not yet established.
