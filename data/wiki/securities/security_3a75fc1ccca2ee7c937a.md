# QUALCOMM Incorporated common stock

## Decision

**Watch; no conviction strategy.** Qualcomm's fiscal Q3 2026 release explains the adverse market
reaction: revenue fell 4%, GAAP diluted EPS fell 23%, handset revenue fell 20%, and management
warned that memory constraints and broad input-cost inflation are pressuring results. Automotive
revenue rose 61% and IoT revenue rose 9%, supporting the diversification thesis, but they did not
offset handset and margin weakness. At the USD 147.61 mark, the shares are below the USD 160 base
case but above the USD 128 margin-of-safety buy zone. The scenario-weighted return is about 8.4%,
entry timing remains weak despite oversold indicators, and no accepted idea relationship exists.

## Business and financial evidence

Fiscal Q3 revenue was USD 9.947 billion, down 4% year over year. Operating income fell 41% to USD
1.626 billion, GAAP diluted EPS fell 23% to USD 1.87, and non-GAAP diluted EPS fell 20% to USD 2.21.
QCT revenue fell 5% to USD 8.504 billion and QCT EBT margin declined four points to 26%; QTL revenue
fell 3% to USD 1.278 billion and QTL EBT margin declined two points to 69%.

The product mix is sharply divergent. QCT handset revenue fell 20% to USD 5.086 billion, while
automotive rose 61% to USD 1.588 billion and IoT rose 9% to USD 1.830 billion. Automotive and IoT
validate Qualcomm's expansion beyond mobile handsets, but handset products still represented about
60% of QCT quarterly revenue and remain the near-term earnings driver. Two customers or licensees
each represented at least 20% of quarterly revenue, reinforcing concentration risk.

Management guided fiscal Q4 revenue to USD 9.7–10.5 billion, QCT revenue to USD 8.4–9.0 billion,
QTL revenue to USD 1.2–1.4 billion, and non-GAAP diluted EPS to USD 2.05–2.25. It also stated that
wafer fabrication, assembly, test, advanced packaging, memory, and other material costs are rising;
pricing actions are expected to help margins only gradually.

Nine-month operating cash flow was USD 8.405 billion and capital expenditure was USD 1.578 billion.
At June 28, cash and marketable securities totaled USD 8.304 billion versus USD 2.489 billion of
short-term debt and USD 12.781 billion of long-term debt. Inventory rose to USD 8.379 billion from
USD 6.526 billion at fiscal year-end, partly reflecting memory supply constraints. Qualcomm had
1.057 billion shares outstanding and returned USD 2.3 billion to stockholders in Q3 through USD
1.4 billion of repurchases and USD 973 million of dividends. Liquidity is adequate, but net debt,
inventory growth, acquisitions, and capital returns limit balance-sheet downside protection.

## Thesis, contrary evidence, catalysts, and invalidation

The constructive thesis is that Qualcomm can use its modem, connectivity, low-power computing,
edge-AI, and patent-licensing advantages to compound high-margin cash generation while automotive,
industrial and consumer IoT, PCs, and data-center products reduce handset dependence. The licensing
business remains highly profitable, and current automotive and IoT growth supports the mechanism.

Contrary evidence is that handset revenue is contracting faster than diversification is currently
growing, segment margins are compressing, and customer concentration remains high. Memory supply,
semiconductor input costs, China and trade restrictions, licensing renewals, OEM insourcing,
competition, acquisition integration, inventory, stock compensation, debt, and legal or regulatory
actions can weaken per-share economics. Management's fiscal 2029 and fiscal 2027 non-handset targets
are forward-looking objectives rather than achieved evidence.

Catalysts are stabilization in handset revenue, realization of pricing actions, sustained automotive
and IoT growth, visible PC and data-center contribution, renewed segment margins, successful product
launches, and continued cash conversion without rising leverage or dilution. Invalidate or materially
downgrade the thesis if handset erosion persists without offsetting non-handset profit, QCT or QTL
margins continue to contract, licensing economics weaken, key customers internalize more silicon,
inventory or debt rises materially, or trade restrictions impair addressable markets.

## Valuation

The selected repository template is `mature_compounder`, using an `earnings_multiple` method. It
normalizes current non-GAAP earnings, the unusually favorable nine-month tax result, investment
gains, stock compensation, acquisition items, net debt, repurchases, and diluted shares. At USD
147.61 and 1.057 billion quarter-end shares, the equity value is about USD 156 billion. The 12-month
scenarios are:

- **Bear (25%): USD 100.00.** USD 8.00 normalized forward EPS at 12.5x as handset contraction,
  customer concentration, input costs, and licensing pressure reduce earnings and the multiple.
  Return: **-32.3%**.
- **Base (50%): USD 160.00.** USD 10.00 normalized forward EPS at 16x as handset revenue stabilizes,
  automotive and IoT growth continue, pricing partly repairs margins, and net debt limits the
  multiple. Return: **+8.4%**.
- **Bull (25%): USD 220.00.** USD 11.00 normalized forward EPS at 20x as non-handset growth
  accelerates, handset and licensing economics stabilize, and QCT margins recover. Return:
  **+49.0%**.

The probability-weighted value is USD 160.00, for about **8.4% expected return** and **6.3%
confidence-adjusted expected return**. The configured 20% margin-of-safety buy-below price is USD
128.00. Expected return, base upside, downside payoff, margin of safety, medium confidence, weak
timing, and the absent accepted relationship prevent the full strategy gate from passing. The
assessment is therefore ineligible for allocation and does not justify conviction strategy research.
