## Economics and thesis

Texas Instruments designs and manufactures analog and embedded-processing semiconductors for
industrial, automotive, data-center, personal-electronics and communications-equipment customers.
Its breadth, long-lived products, broad channels and internally owned manufacturing reduce reliance
on any single design win. Management says 300mm production costs about 40% less per chip than 200mm
production, while internal capacity improves supply control; the trade-off is substantial fixed cost
and utilization sensitivity through a semiconductor cycle.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose 23% year over year and 13% sequentially to USD 5.463 billion, led
by industrial, data center and automotive. Gross margin rose to 61.4%, operating margin reached
42.3%, and diluted EPS rose 52% to USD 2.14. Analog revenue rose 26% to USD 4.365 billion and
Embedded Processing revenue rose 16% to USD 788 million. Third-quarter guidance calls for USD
5.65-6.15 billion of revenue and USD 2.23-2.57 of EPS.

Trailing-12-month operating cash flow was USD 8.667 billion. Company-defined free cash flow was USD
6.534 billion after USD 3.312 billion of capital expenditure and USD 1.179 billion of CHIPS Act
proceeds. The thesis is that broad cyclical recovery and rising factory loading allow TI's analog
breadth and 300mm cost advantage to compound cash flow. The counter-thesis is that investors are
paying a premium before the cycle, new capacity and acquisition financing have proved their
incremental returns.
