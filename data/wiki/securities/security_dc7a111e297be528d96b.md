# Tesla: business lines, production and valuation

**Research cutoff: October 20, 2026, before Q3 earnings. All dollar amounts are USD.** This is a current research dossier, revised after methodological review of its first October 20 valuation. Reported results, company claims, analyst consensus and independent scenarios are identified separately. Maintain the business-line register and the production/valuation tables on future refreshes; a short earnings summary should not replace them. Missing disclosure remains missing, rather than becoming zero.

## Investment assessment

**The central result is conditional, not a price prediction:** Tesla can achieve substantial vehicle, storage and autonomy growth without necessarily producing enough distributable cash to justify the market price. Conversely, better unit economics, a longer profitable growth runway or lower required returns can produce much larger values. The useful questions are which outcomes are economically viable, what funds them, and what evidence would change the assumptions.

This revision replaces the previous one-year terminal-multiple SOTP and $20/$35/$50 billion funding allowances with annual operating cash flows for 2027–2040, an explicit June-to-December 2026 cash bridge, and a reinvestment-supported terminal value. It separates company-owned robotaxis from the partner platform, tests partner returns, and reconciles non-operating assets. It is an **illustrative operating DCF, not an audited integrated balance-sheet forecast**. Product economics, post-2031 growth, taxes and capital efficiency remain uncertain. More accounting structure does not create operating evidence.

The 2031 base retains 3.5 million passenger sales, 250 GWh of storage and one million average active robotaxis. Those are already demanding successes, not conservative observed run rates. The partner commission is now 20%, rather than 30%, because the explicit operator-cost test leaves almost no return at 30%. Optimus commercialization is conditional on evidence gates. A nine-year transition after 2031 avoids treating that expansion year as automatically mature.

Scenario outputs, their full annual cash flows and sensitivity tables appear below. The probability-weighted figure is an output of **one subjective set of weights**, not the central estimate of intrinsic value. Low confidence keeps this instrument Unrated in the current publication rules. There is no supported prediction of a percentage price fall or 12-month convergence. The assessment record uses a 63-month operational milestone horizon to December 2031; the cash-flow projection extends through 2040 and all scenario values are discounted to October 2026.

The last completed-session price in PaperTrader remains $368.06 on October 19, from its existing canonical feed. ARK's $4,400 December-2031 target is an external, more optimistic model, not an input to this DCF. Its workbook audit remains below. [S2, S3]

| Tesla quarterly update | Reported operations, installed capacity and management plans | Audited output at nameplate capacity |
| October 19 board letter | Tesla's latest claims in a proxy-solicitation document | Independent verification, order backlog or project profitability |
| Company-compiled consensus | A dated sample of external analyst estimates | Tesla guidance or realized results |
| ARK article and workbook | A specific investor's assumptions and modeled outcomes | Consensus, empirically calibrated success probabilities or guaranteed returns |
| Independent model here | Reproducible scenario arithmetic and explicit judgment | A forecast precise enough to eliminate execution uncertainty |

The board letter reports Cybercab deployment, Robotaxi in seven US cities, Megapack 3 production in Texas, more than 130 GWh cumulative storage deployments, nearly 1.5 million paid FSD customers, over 15 billion cumulative FSD miles, more than 10 million cumulative vehicle deliveries, Semi/Optimus manufacturing work and Terafab. These are company claims as of October 19. The letter asks shareholders to support the board at the December 3 annual meeting. It supplies neither a production ramp nor a segment valuation. [S1]

Keep **annual vs cumulative**, **production vs deliveries**, **factory capacity vs realized output**, **paid vs supervised vs driverless miles**, and **paying subscriptions vs cumulative vehicle base** separate. Seven cities can include different operating permissions and service areas. The Q2 deck specifically described the Bay Area service as using a safety driver; a city count is not proof that all service is driverless. [S4]

## Business economics and thesis tests

### Passenger vehicles: protect unit economics while funding expansion

Tesla's core advantage could come from manufacturing scale, integrated software, charging access and cost reduction. The principal economic challenge is converting these into gross profit after incentives, financing subsidies, logistics, warranty and mix changes. Lower sticker prices can increase addressable demand while reducing contribution per vehicle. Global volume growth does not establish regional competitiveness or pricing power.

Separate Model 3/Y demand from Cybertruck utilization and legacy Model S/X obligations. Track geographic registration evidence, exports and inventory where comparable data becomes available. Do not equate factory utilization with market share, or an order deposit with an enforceable sale. Competition from other EV manufacturers matters through realized price and product mix, rather than through an unsupported blanket market-share assumption.

**Sensitivity:** a $1,000 ASP change at 3.5 million units changes annual revenue by $3.5 billion. Its effect on cash value depends on price elasticity, manufacturing cost and reinvestment; it is not assigned an automatic EV/EBIT multiple. The integrated operating stresses below distinguish price changes from cost changes.

**Thesis test:** volume growth accompanied by stable ex-credit contribution profit and manageable warranty cost supports the base. Sustained discount-driven growth with weak cash conversion does not. Battery/pack expansion is valuable only when economically saleable output follows.

### Semi: a separate commercial fleet proposition

Semi buyers evaluate total cost per productive mile, uptime, payload, charging downtime and service coverage. Acquisition price alone is inadequate. A route with predictable depot charging can have different economics from long-haul operation with unreliable high-power infrastructure. Fleet orders, prototypes, delivered trucks and revenue recognition must be separately tracked.

The model assumes $6.4 billion base 2031 revenue from 40,000 deliveries at $160,000 and a 10% EBIT margin. These are independent assumptions because verified production economics are not disclosed in the reviewed material. The high case of 80,000 annual deliveries would require expansion beyond commonly discussed initial-scale ambitions and should not be mistaken for installed capacity. Autonomous trucking is not separately capitalized: it requires its own safety, operational and regulatory proof.

### FSD software: paying users matter more than cumulative miles

FSD revenue can grow through higher attach rates, wider approved availability and retention. But supervised-driver software and a driverless commercial service have different liabilities, user value and permitted operation. The Q2 deck reports 1.48 million active FSD subscriptions; the board letter's nearly 1.5 million paid customers is not necessarily the same definition or a fresh quarterly average. [S4, S1]

The independent revenue equation is average paying users × realized annual revenue per user. The base 5 million × $1,000 yields $5 billion. The 2031 base EBIT-margin assumption is 60%; its cash value now depends on the annual ramp and reinvestment model, rather than a separate 30× earnings multiple. This deliberately allocates meaningful development/support cost. Do not multiply all cumulative deliveries by a US subscription list price or add upfront license revenue to subscriptions without separating users and accounting periods.

OEM licensing remains a tracked option with zero incremental base valuation until a material agreement, eligible hardware, commercial pricing and responsibility for failures are established. Connectivity and other paid features remain in residual services. This avoids counting the same software revenue three times.

### Robotaxi: the fleet operator must earn a return too

The revised model has two businesses. Tesla-owned cars earn the entire fare and bear fleet acquisition, depreciation and all operating costs. Partner-owned cars earn Tesla a commission, while the partner must earn an adequate return after its own costs. Recognized revenue below is an economic modeling convention, not a forecast of GAAP principal/agent treatment. No fleet purchase is counted as an external automotive sale; captive charging is not external charging revenue.

At the base 45,000 paid miles/year and $1.10/mile, gross fares are $49,500/car. A 70% paid-mile share implies about 64,286 total miles/year, or 176/day, of which 123/day are paid. This is a 2031 operating assumption, not today's utilization. Competition can lower fares and utilization together; the joint stress below tests that.

For an owned car, the base deducts $0.35 per total mile for energy, maintenance and insurance; $7,000/year for cleaning, depot and field operations; $5,000 depreciation on a $25,000 five-year asset; and $0.08 per paid mile for platform, remote support and computing operations. These are explicit judgmental cost allowances, not measured Tesla costs. No salvage value is assumed. Fleet purchase/replacement capex is separate in the cash-flow table; depreciation is added back exactly once.

A partner pays 20% of fares to Tesla. Its $39,600 retained revenue less $22,500 variable operating costs, $7,000 fixed costs and $5,000 depreciation leaves $5,100 operating profit before financing and tax, about 20.4% of initial vehicle cost. At a 30% commission, profit drops to $150, or 0.6% of cost. That is not a sufficiently attractive standalone return to assume a willing scaled supply of partners. Financing, city fees or greater downtime can erase even the 20%-commission return. Tesla's partner-platform cost includes $0.08/paid mile and $1,000/car/year support, plus its allocated network development cost below.

The base 50/50 ownership split therefore recognizes 60% of bookings, not the former 65%. Annual network-wide development and central expense is deducted separately, reaching $1.5 billion in 2031. The model does not apply a single 30% margin to the combined revenues.

NHTSA's September 4 Cybercab self-certification query is a potential ramp/retrofit risk, not a finding of noncompliance. [S10] Waymo reports more than 500,000 autonomous trips/week; this establishes commercial competition, not matched-route profitability. [S11] The stress cases lower fares and utilization and extend the ramp; a specific recall cost is not invented without a fleet and remediation scope.

**Evidence required:** average active vehicles by city and permission type; paid and empty miles; safety and remote-intervention denominators; insurance costs; fleet replacement lives; all-in operator returns; permissioned service expansion; and partner renewals. Factory capacity and cumulative test miles cannot substitute for these measures.

### Optimus: value productive work, not demonstrations

An economically useful humanoid must reliably complete a task in the customer's environment, integrate with existing processes and recover from exceptions at a support cost the customer can afford. Generality may expand the market but can also make deployment and safety more difficult. Factory training units and external revenue-producing robots are different populations.

The base valuation uses **100,000 annual hardware sales × $25,000**, plus **200,000 average paying robots × $12,000 annual software**, for **$4.9 billion total revenue**. The base applies a 15% EBIT contribution margin and then charges $0.5 billion/year of dedicated development expense. The bull uses 600,000 hardware sales and 1.2 million average paying robots for $27.6 billion revenue. Its paid base is corrected from 1.5 million: scaling the published base sales path sixfold yields 1.5 million cumulative deliveries at year-end, but only 1.2 million average active units in 2031. These are adoption assumptions, not bookings. Internal cost savings are embedded in manufacturing margins; internal hardware/software transfers are eliminated. A $0.5 billion annual development charge continues even in the no-external-sales case; zero sales do not mean free failure.

A customer ROI test should compare avoided labor cost at actual task productivity with annual software fees, hardware depreciation, maintenance, integration, power and supervision. Two shifts of theoretical labor substitution should not be priced as two fully eliminated jobs without considering downtime, redeployment of workers and task coverage. Useful pilot evidence includes paid renewals, hours between interventions, task success under changing conditions, installation effort and customer payback. Unit production alone is insufficient.

**Commercialization gates:** (1) paid task-specific deployment and independently described acceptance criteria; (2) positive customer ROI after hardware depreciation, annual fees, maintenance, integration and supervision; (3) repeat orders and paid renewals with measured intervention rates; (4) serviceable production scale and positive cash conversion. Missing gates keep the modeled business conditional. The no-commercialization stress removes revenue and its contribution profit while retaining development costs; it does not pretend failure costs zero.

### Energy storage: an established business with a difficult scale-up

Megapack and Powerwall sell systems and services into distinct utility/commercial and residential channels. Revenue depends on deployed GWh, system price, project scope and recognition timing. Cell prices can fall while system gross margins improve, but aggressive system-price declines can also transfer most savings to customers. Backlog should be evaluated for cancellation rights, deposits, customer financing, interconnection timing and supplier availability.

The base **250 GWh × $130/kWh** produces **$32.5 billion revenue**. The base assumes 16% allocated EBIT margin. The financial bridge below makes the required system-cost reduction explicit; no separate 24× EBIT multiple is applied. This includes Powerwall and energy software economics and excludes the separate small solar/generation bucket. The system-price input must not be compared directly with a bare-cell quote.

Monitor cell procurement, local content, tariffs, warranties, fire/safety obligations and working capital. Project delays can move deployments between quarters without changing long-term demand, but repeated slippage or rising receivables can signal a cash-conversion problem. For residential storage, installer capacity and utility approval can be bottlenecks even when factory supply is adequate.

Autobidder, VPP participation and Tesla Electric may improve customer economics or add recurring income. The value belongs to Tesla only to the extent of retained fees/spreads after customer payouts, energy procurement, collateral, operating costs and risk. Gross market electricity transactions are not equivalent to Tesla revenue. Without separate segment disclosure, adding a software valuation on top of the storage margin would duplicate some benefits.

### Charging, insurance, service and solar

Charging has a potentially valuable network position, but connector count is only capacity. Revenue requires delivered kWh and net spread after electricity, demand charges, site rent, maintenance and payment costs. Q2 reported 8,704 stations and 82,357 connectors. [S4] The base assumes $5 billion external charging revenue, 18% EBIT margin and 25× EBIT. Captive robotaxi charging is included in fleet costs, not booked as an external sale.

Insurance should be assessed with underwriting measures: claims severity, loss and expense ratios, reserves and reinsurance. More premium volume can destroy value if pricing is inadequate. Repairs, used vehicles and parts have different margins and working-capital requirements from software. The residual-services base assumes $20 billion revenue, 8% EBIT margin and 18× EBIT, excluding charging and FSD. It must be reconciled to future reported categories rather than simply added to Tesla's entire services segment.

Solar/generation has a separate modest base of $2 billion revenue, 8% EBIT margin and 15× EBIT. Energy software and VPP value is already included elsewhere. Roadster, Robovan and speculative third-party AI-chip sales receive no separate premium without commercial evidence. Reuters reports a Roadster production objective of 2028; no firm pricing was disclosed at the unveiling. [S12]

### Batteries, compute and Terafab: enabling assets with real costs

Cells, packs, cathode production, refining, inference chips and training infrastructure may improve supply security and unit costs. The appropriate initial valuation treatment is through downstream margins, production availability and capital requirements. Internal transfer revenue creates no consolidated economic value. An external semiconductor business would require customers, competitive cost/yield, usable capacity and a contractual allocation of profits.

Terafab's strategic appeal does not establish Tesla's ownership of all related assets or returns. Track legal ownership, funding obligations, counterparties, technology rights, power procurement, construction milestones, yield and transfer pricing. Dojo/Cortex/chip design are tracked as capabilities and costs; there is no separate assumed cloud-computing revenue stream. No value from a hypothetical Tesla–SpaceX merger is credited without signed terms. ### Independent checks of the implied economics

**Production growth.** ARK's 10.2 million 2031 vehicle sales versus its 1.6 million 2025 starting point requires about **36.2% annual growth over six years**. Its storage path from 47 to 2,278 GWh requires about **91% annual growth**. The latter is roughly **15.3 times** the analyst survey's 2030 storage expectation, albeit with one additional year. It is an industrial-capacity and market-absorption thesis, not simply incremental improvement in an existing factory.

**Storage price/volume combination.** The article's base storage revenue of $163 billion divided by 2,278 GWh implies about **$71.6/kWh system revenue**. High unit growth must coexist with dramatic price compression, adequate supplier economics and useful margins. At a hypothetical 50-GWh factory module, 2,278 GWh requires the equivalent of approximately **46 fully utilized modules**, before allowing for downtime or yield. The 50-GWh module is a sizing assumption, not an assertion of Tesla's current installed capacity.

**Robotaxi partner viability.** At the saved simulation's approximately 74,200 miles/year and $0.182 owner fee, a partner receives around **$13,500 per vehicle/year** before its costs. A simple stress test assumes $25,000 vehicle cost, five-year life and zero residual, giving $5,000 annual depreciation. At 0.25 kWh/mile and $0.15/kWh, electricity adds roughly $2,783 at 74,200 total miles. That leaves about $5,700 for insurance, cleaning, maintenance, financing, downtime, overhead and owner profit, before extra empty miles. These are independent illustrative costs, not observed fleet expenses. Lower hardware cost, favorable energy rates or different contractual cost allocation could improve the result. The model needs a credible operator contract and cost allocation to substantiate such a small fleet-owner fee.

**Robot subscription pricing.** A $174,223 annual fee can consume most of the theoretical savings from two $90,000 labor shifts before hardware, servicing and integration. Customer willingness to pay should be constrained by verified net productivity and ROI. A technical breakthrough could create a large market, but it does not automatically give Tesla the entire avoided wage bill. The independent model uses $12,000 annual software revenue, an assumption to test rather than a forecast of Tesla pricing.

**Dependence and downside.** Drawing many inputs does not diversify common risks. Slow autonomy could simultaneously reduce fleet adoption, utilization, funding cash, robot training and investor multiples. A proper stress case needs correlated setbacks, redesign/recall costs, partner failures and financing dilution. The workbook's zero floors for negative segment EBITDA and its positive-growth ranges should be examined when interpreting downside. A 25th percentile inside a highly successful future is not a 25% probability of commercial failure.

**Time value.** Discounting $4,400 for 5.2 years gives approximately **$2,680 at 10%**, **$2,441 at 12%** or **$2,127 at 15%**, before independently changing its operating assumptions. This is merely a time-value translation of ARK's target; it is not this dossier's fair value. The much lower independent base valuation mainly comes from economics and deployment assumptions, not discounting alone.

## Annual operating cash flows and conditional equity values

### What changed and what remains uncertain

The previous $14.59/$100.03/$610.35 outputs and $176.46 weighted figure are retired. They came from a 2031 EBIT-multiple SOTP with judgmental funding and dilution allowances. This is a change of model, not an earnings update or a mechanical repricing. The earlier arithmetic was correct; the unsupported link between growth and funding was the weakness.

All estimates below are **original assumptions**, not management guidance or analyst consensus. The annual model includes segment sales and margins, owned-fleet costs and acquisition cohorts, taxes, depreciation, non-fleet capital spending and working capital through 2031. From 2032 it uses a disclosed aggregate transition and incremental-return framework. It is deliberately not described as a fully integrated three-statement forecast: debt maturities, exact tax jurisdictions, restricted cash and the award vesting ledger are not modeled.

### Accounting, financing and time conventions

- Dollar amounts are billions unless a table says otherwise. Explicit years are 2027–2040; end-year discounting first values operations at December 2026 and discounts the resulting equity value another 0.2 years to October 2026. This is an approximate research-date convention, not a 12-month price target.
- **FCFF = EBIT − cash taxes + D&A − capex − change in operating working capital.** EBIT includes depreciation and compensation expense. Manufacturing depreciation already in cost of revenue is not deducted again when bridging gross profit to EBIT.
- Cash taxes are 25% of positive EBIT; losses receive no immediate tax credit and no carryforward benefit is modeled. This is a conservative simplification, not a prediction of Tesla's statutory or effective tax rate. Existing deferred tax assets are not separately capitalized.
- Segment margins include ordinary central expense and cash-equivalent compensation. Dedicated robotaxi network expense and $0.5 billion annual Optimus development are shown separately. There is no second generic corporate charge. Keeping compensation as an expense without adding back SBC treats future compensation as cash replacement and avoids counting both an SBC add-back and unchanged ownership.
- Use a common **12% nominal USD unlevered discount rate** across scenarios so operating upside is not amplified by an automatic lower rate. Illustrative construction: 4% risk-free assumption + 1.6 business-risk beta × 5% equity-risk-premium assumption = 12%. These are valuation judgments, not observed October yields or an estimated beta. No financing tax-shield premium is included. Rate sensitivity is published below; scenario probabilities also remain subjective.
- The denominator is **4.1 billion effective existing shares in every case**: 3.949 billion reported June issued shares plus a rounded 0.151 billion allowance for existing awards. This is not a verified fully diluted award count. Future cash-equivalent compensation is expensed, so an additional blanket future-SBC dilution allowance would count it twice. Exact award terms remain a refresh requirement. [S5]
- Enterprise DCF already charges negative operating cash flows. Future debt/equity funding is assumed available on fair-value, zero-NPV terms: do not add financing proceeds as business value or divide by a speculative future share count after already deducting the same cash shortfall. At fair issue price, the additional investor claim offsets the proceeds. The liquidity screen below identifies funding pressure; distressed issuance, fees, covenants and excess required cash can lower current-holder value and are not hidden inside a fabricated dilution schedule.

### Transition to a sustainable terminal state

For 2032–2040, all businesses are aggregated. Revenue growth falls linearly from the first rate below in 2032 to perpetual growth in 2040. The combined 2031 EBIT margin declines by 10% **relative**, linearly over nine years; the resulting 2040 margin remains constant thereafter. This prevents indefinitely capitalizing peak ramp margins, but is still a judgment. Business composition is held proportional; it is not a separate post-2031 fleet forecast.

Annual incremental reinvestment is `max(NOPAT_t − NOPAT_(t−1), 0) / incremental ROIC`. D&A retains its 2031 share of revenue; working capital remains 8%. Set `capex = D&A + incremental reinvestment − change in working capital`. Thus FCFF equals NOPAT less required reinvestment; D&A is not a free cash-flow benefit. No asset-release windfall is credited if profit shrinks. Terminal next-year FCFF is `NOPAT_2040 × (1+g) × (1−g/ROIC)`, and terminal EV is that FCFF divided by `(12%−g)`.

A half-sized fleet path is a persistent adoption shortfall, not the same as a two-year delay. For a pure delay stress, shift the entire 2027–2040 operating FCFF stream and terminal value two years later, keep the same December bridge, and add $0.8 billion network plus $0.5 billion Optimus annual development cash expense in each waiting year. That defined two-year common-delay stress produces **$26.23/share**. Approval timing should ultimately be modeled by city and affected business when evidence permits. A redesign reserve can then be based on actual fleet exposure and remediation scope rather than an arbitrary valuation haircut.

A 30% incremental haircut/tax on the carried $3.681 billion non-operating assets reduces base value by **$0.26/share**. An additional $10 billion December cash obligation reduces it by **$2.38/share**. These last two are bridge-only stresses, not full operating reruns.

### What would be required to reach the market price?

The $368.06 reference is a comparison, not a calibration target. To make reverse valuation reproducible, hold all base inputs fixed except the named input and solve the same DCF. Do not interpret the result as an investor survey or as a feasible forecast. A lower discount rate cannot by itself establish a competitive advantage, and multiplying fleet scale without congestion or price response is only an arithmetic boundary.


With base fares, utilization, commission, owner economics and fixed network development otherwise unchanged, the model needs approximately **42.9 million average active robotaxis in 2031** to reach $368.06; it proportionally scales the earlier fleet path and its purchases. That extrapolation is far beyond the base and may violate demand, supply and competition constraints. It is not a new forecast. Other combinations of stronger FSD, better manufacturing economics, longer growth duration and lower required returns can also bridge the gap. No single reverse-solved fleet figure proves what the market believes.

### Model limitations and interpretation

Established vehicles, storage, charging and services have operating evidence; future margins and reinvestment still require judgment. FSD has paying users but uncertain scalable economics. Robotaxi and Optimus commercialization are conditional opportunities with development costs even when they fail. The no-commercialization stresses expose that distinction without pretending that a business can be removed without changing shared costs or strategic decisions.

The model cannot establish a reliable intrinsic-value point estimate. Its weaknesses now sit in identifiable assumptions: cost per mile, partner returns, paid robot adoption, allocated segment costs, top-down capex, aggregate post-2031 transition, fair financing and award treatment. Actual city economics, plant budgets, tax attributes and existing-award terms can materially change it. The fact that cash-flow arithmetic reconciles does not validate those assumptions.


## Risks, catalysts and conditions that change the thesis

| Area | Evidence that would strengthen valuation | Evidence that would weaken valuation |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicles | Profitable volume growth, durable net ASP, better yield and pack supply | Incentive-led sales, warranty escalation, persistent underutilization |
| Storage | Deployments and cash receipts grow together with stable system margin | Project deferrals, pricing pressure, warranty/safety costs, supplier bottlenecks |
| FSD | Paying-user growth, retention and contribution profit | Churn, price cuts, legal restrictions, hardware remediation costs |
| Robotaxi | Permissioned scale, high paid utilization, transparent all-in costs and viable partners | Delays, costly interventions, poor unit contribution, fleet/insurance losses |
| Optimus | Paid repeat deployments with measurable customer ROI | Demonstrations without repeat customers, low task reliability, intensive supervision |
| Capital | Operating cash funds expansion while productivity rises | Repeated cash burn beyond the annual path, insufficient liquidity or financing on unfavorable terms |
| Governance | Clear related-party terms, ownership and economic rights | Unfavorable transfers, uncertain funding obligations, executive/key-person disruption |

**Invalidate the independent base downward** if Tesla cannot approach the modeled commercial adoption while holding the assumed margins and funding needs, or if shared regulatory/technical problems impair several businesses together. Material cash burn beyond the annual forecast requires immediate revaluation even if long-term production ambitions are unchanged.

**Invalidate it upward** if observed paid robotaxi and Optimus deployments demonstrate sustainably higher cash contribution and lower capital intensity, with evidence of customer/partner returns and scalable permission to operate. A larger total addressable market, a product demonstration or an announced factory is insufficient by itself.

Near-term catalysts are the October 21 financial release, clearer 2027 capital requirements, product/fleet ramp disclosure, regulatory developments and the December shareholder meeting. ## Refresh checklist and unresolved evidence

At each earnings release, replace forecasts only with matched-period actuals, maintain the full business register, and update the following in order:

1. Reconcile revenue, gross profit, EBIT, OCF, capex, cash, debt, minority claims and share counts. Keep non-operating gains separate.
2. Reconcile vehicle production/deliveries by disclosed category, inventory and storage deployments. Maintain factory capacity with source date and definition.
3. Update average paying FSD users and realized revenue; distinguish paid owners from fleet subscriptions.
4. Record robotaxi cities, exact permission/driver status, average active fleet, paid miles and all-in economics. Track owned vs partner assets and replacement needs.
5. Separate Optimus factory/training builds, external sales, active paid deployments and verified labor savings. Avoid year-end fleet counts in full-year revenue calculations.
6. Reassess Semi customer uptime/charging and product economics. Keep passenger and commercial production separate.
7. Track Powerwall, solar, energy software, VPP, insurance, charging and service through their specific KPI rows even where data remains unreported.
8. Update the cost/funding/ownership evidence for battery materials, compute and Terafab. Do not add an internal-revenue business to SOTP.
9. Recompute annual cash flows, capital needs, existing-award shares, subjective probabilities and joint sensitivities; record source dates and source hashes. Preserve immutable security/issuer IDs.

**Open evidence gaps:** audited standalone robotaxi/FSD/Optimus earnings; production and paid deployment by new product; average paid miles and fleet utilization; full fleet-owner contracts and costs; external robot pricing and ROI; product-level storage margin; capex split and return by project; precise future award dilution; and attributable economics of semiconductor/related-party projects. These prevent a high-confidence point valuation. The page nevertheless tracks the opportunities rather than collapsing them into an undifferentiated AI premium.
