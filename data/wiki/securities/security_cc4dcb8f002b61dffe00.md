## Economics and thesis

Parrot designs professional micro-drones and image-analysis products. Q1 2026 revenue was EUR 27.9
million, up 52% year over year. Professional micro-UAV revenue doubled to EUR 21.3 million and
represented 76% of the group, driven by the ANAFI UKR production ramp and institutional demand.
Critical-infrastructure revenue declined 16% to EUR 6.6 million as the business shifted toward
subscriptions.

The operating evidence is improving: 2025 revenue reached EUR 79.8 million and operations broke
even in the second half; management said Q1 was slightly profitable and did not consume cash.
Recent NATO Support and Procurement Agency and Finnish defence announcements indicate real
procurement routes. They do not, however, disclose enough order value, customer concentration,
component country of origin, gross margin, or recurring economics to treat Parrot as a proven
trusted-supply-chain compounder.

## Valuation, catalysts, and risks

No supportable valuation is recorded before the July 31 half-year release. The repository has no
validated current mark, and the available evidence does not provide a complete diluted-share,
net-cash, segment-margin, customer-concentration, or backlog bridge. A later model must reconcile
those inputs and use downside cases for procurement timing and working capital.

Catalysts are disclosed defence order conversion, sustained positive operating cash, improving
gross margin, auditable allied-component content, and recurring critical-infrastructure revenue.
Invalidate if orders stay immaterial, institutional concentration rises, supply constraints absorb
cash, trusted-source qualifications cannot be substantiated, or pricing and scale fail to produce
durable margins.
