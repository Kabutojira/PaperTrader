## Business economics

For the six months ended June 30, 2026, Parrot reported revenue of **EUR 57.3 million**, up 71%. Professional micro-UAV revenue reached **EUR 42.1 million**, up 134%, and represented roughly three quarters of group revenue. Management attributed the increase to ANAFI UKR and reported deliveries in the United States, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Finland and France.

Critical-infrastructure solutions generated **EUR 15.1 million**, down 3% at current exchange rates and flat at constant rates. The business is moving from perpetual licenses toward subscriptions, which may improve recurring revenue over time but can create quarterly volatility.

Gross profit was **EUR 40.8 million**, or a **71.2% margin**, versus 74.8% a year earlier. Management explained the decline as a mix effect: ANAFI UKR is scaling but has not achieved mature-product margins, while the structurally higher-margin infrastructure business contributed less. Current operating income was **EUR 2.6 million**, including EUR 2.5 million of non-recurring insurance proceeds. Micro-UAVs contributed EUR 5.5 million of current operating income; critical-infrastructure solutions lost EUR 1.2 million.

These results demonstrate operating leverage and differentiated products, but not yet a long record of durable profitability or free-cash-flow conversion.

This gives Parrot financing flexibility, but dependence on shareholder support and expected H2 cash utilization keep liquidity below a fortress level.

## Scenario drivers

### Bear

Institutional deliveries slow after the initial procurement wave, Q4 comparisons become difficult, and ANAFI UKR remains below mature-product margins. Working capital absorbs cash and the business receives no net-cash valuation credit.

### Base

Micro-UAV growth continues at a moderated rate, underlying operations remain near break-even or improve gradually, and H2 cash use is manageable. PIX4D's subscription transition progresses without a prolonged decline.

### Bull

Follow-on ANAFI UKR orders and new tenders produce strong revenue, manufacturing scale improves margins, CHUCK 3.0 reaches commercial deployment, and PIX4D subscriptions strengthen recurring economics. Cash use remains controlled and dilution is limited.

## Catalysts, downside and invalidation

Catalysts include repeat ANAFI UKR deliveries, additional institutional tenders, improving production economics, CHUCK 3.0 commercialization, PIX4D subscription growth and evidence that working-capital needs remain controlled. The scheduled October 28 Q3 release is the nearest identified catalyst.

Downside centers on irregular government procurement, product and program concentration, immature ANAFI UKR margins, manufacturing execution and working-capital absorption. Rapid technical change and potential dilution also matter. Balance-sheet capacity is scored separately and is not deducted twice from the residual-risk assessment.

The positive thesis would be invalidated if micro-UAV revenue contracts persistently, contract awards fail to become repeat deliveries, underlying operations return to sustained losses, or cash consumption cannot be explained by temporary growth-related working capital. Material dilution or a changed share count would require a new per-share valuation.
