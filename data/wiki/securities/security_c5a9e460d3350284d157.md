## Economics and thesis

Current primary evidence confirms that Valero captured the 2026 product-market dislocation. Second-quarter refining operating income rose to USD 4.47 billion from USD 1.27 billion, refining margin rose to USD 6.34 billion from USD 3.28 billion, and throughput averaged 2.95 million barrels per day versus 2.92 million. Gulf Coast diesel less Brent averaged USD 43.52 per barrel versus USD 14.79, while regional gasoline and diesel indicators were not uniform. The mechanism is therefore real but cyclical rather than a durable structural growth rate.

Execution was broadly reliable, although the Port Arthur outage reduced throughput before normal rates resumed during Q2 and Benicia refining operations ceased by the end of April. Renewable Diesel operating income of USD 717 million and Ethanol operating income of USD 318 million also benefited consolidated results, so petroleum refining alone should not be used to annualize peak quarterly earnings.

## Catalysts, risks, and invalidation

Catalysts are persistent product-stock tightness, delayed refinery restarts, reliable high utilization, favorable crude differentials, St. Charles FCC optimization, and cash returns after sustaining investment. Risks are rapid crack normalization, demand destruction, refinery outages, turnaround and catalyst costs, renewable-fuel policy and RIN costs, California exit obligations, working-capital reversal, crude-differential changes, debt and lease claims, and capital returns made against peak earnings.

Invalidate the positive cyclical thesis if regional realized margins normalize without offsetting cost or volume improvement, throughput reliability deteriorates, sustaining and turnaround needs consume normalized operating cash, net leverage rises materially, or product inventories and competing refinery capacity recover faster than expected.
