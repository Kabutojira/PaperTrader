## Business economics

Enphase sells semiconductor-based microinverters, batteries, and energy-management systems. It is
a direct residential solar and distributed-storage exposure, with additional optionality from
commercial products and a solid-state-transformer platform under development for data centres.
Near-term economics remain highly sensitive to installer demand, financing, tax policy, tariffs,
channel inventory, and the mix of safe-harbour shipments and manufacturing tax credits.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was USD 282.9 million, down from USD 343.3 million in the prior quarter
and USD 356.1 million a year earlier. US revenue fell approximately 23% sequentially after the
Section 25D residential credit expired and because of seasonality; US sell-through fell 48%
sequentially and 18% year over year. Europe revenue rose about 36% sequentially. Battery shipments
were 103.1 MWh, down from 150.1 MWh in the fourth quarter.

GAAP gross margin was 35.5%, GAAP operating loss was USD 29.6 million, and non-GAAP gross margin was
43.9%, including a 4.3 percentage-point tariff impact. Operating cash flow was USD 102.9 million and
company-defined free cash flow was USD 83.0 million. Cash, equivalents, and marketable securities
were USD 930.6 million after the company paid USD 632.5 million to settle its 2026 convertible notes;
remaining debt carrying value was USD 572.5 million.

## Thesis

Enphase has differentiated positive exposure to distributed solar, storage, and power-management
software, and its net-cash liquidity and current free-cash-flow generation make it financially
stronger than a simple distressed-survivor narrative. The reset is not yet confirmed: underlying US
sell-through contracted sharply, residential incentives weakened, financing remains restrictive,
and safe-harbour shipments and tax-credit monetisation complicate the quality of reported revenue
and cash flow.

## Valuation

No supportable valuation range is recorded. The deterministic market cache has a 29 July 2026
adjusted close of USD 35.07, but Q2 does not yet provide normalised owner earnings. Safe-harbour
revenue represented USD 84.3 million of USD 291.9 million quarterly revenue, a USD 45.4 million
tariff refund lifted GAAP gross margin by 15.6 percentage points, and six-month stock compensation
was USD 92.5 million. A single reported-EPS or trailing-sales multiple would therefore conceal the
policy, refund, dilution, and demand mix. A later valuation should separate core sell-through from
policy-timed revenue, remove nonrecurring refunds, use the diluted share count, and scenario-test
normalised GAAP operating margin and free cash flow.

## Catalysts and confirmation

- US sell-through stabilising sequentially without additional channel inventory;
- battery shipments and attach rates growing at healthy gross margin;
- Q2 revenue landing above the midpoint with less dependence on safe-harbour shipments;
- tariffs declining or pricing and domestic production offsetting their margin impact;
- European growth broadening and commercial products converting into recurring demand; and
- sustained free cash flow after normalising tax-credit monetisation and working capital.

## Risks and invalidation

Invalidate the positive case if US sell-through continues to contract, financing or lost incentives
prevent residential demand recovery, safe-harbour commitments fail to become end-customer demand,
inventory or purchase obligations force discounting, tariffs compress gross margin, battery growth
stalls, warranties exceed reserves, or cash is allocated without adequate return.
