## Business and financial evidence

Quanta provides infrastructure solutions across electric power, transmission, substations,
distribution, interconnections, load centers, communications, underground utilities, pipelines,
and industrial facilities. That makes it a direct construction and skilled-labor channel for
[[ideas/idea_ai_infrastructure_power|AI infrastructure and power bottlenecks]], while demand from
utilities and broader grid modernization is more diversified than the data-center theme alone.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose to USD 9.56 billion from USD 6.77 billion, adjusted diluted EPS
rose to USD 4.24 from USD 2.48, operating cash flow reached USD 1.1 billion, and free cash flow
reached USD 0.9 billion. Total remaining performance obligations reached USD 33.6 billion and total
backlog reached USD 53.4 billion. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to USD 39.3-39.7
billion, adjusted diluted-EPS guidance to USD 16.45-16.95, and free-cash-flow guidance to USD
2.00-2.50 billion.

Backlog quality is not equivalent to guaranteed revenue. Quanta includes estimated MSA renewals and
non-fixed-price work in backlog, and its filing says cancellations, delays, regulatory requirements,
weather, commercial issues, and change-order acceptance can alter timing and realization. Fixed-price
contracts, skilled-labor availability and inflation, customer collection, permitting, supply-chain
constraints, and acquisition integration remain material execution risks.

At June 30, Quanta held USD 506.4 million cash against USD 683.0 million current debt and USD 5.422
billion long-term debt. Current assets of USD 11.860 billion exceeded current liabilities of USD
10.798 billion, and first-half operating cash flow was USD 1.487 billion. However, goodwill rose to
USD 7.869 billion after further acquisitions, so acquisition performance, leverage, and impairment
risk continue to reduce balance-sheet resilience.

## Thesis, contrary evidence, and invalidation

The constructive thesis is that grid hardening, electrification, generation and transmission
investment, large-load interconnections, and data-center electrical work create a long-duration
project pipeline that Quanta can serve with scarce craft labor and integrated solutions. Record RPO,
backlog, Electric growth, margin improvement, and higher guidance show that demand is converting to
reported earnings and cash.

Contrary evidence is the gap between backlog and firm RPO, dependence on project timing and contract
estimates, fixed-price cost risk, skilled-labor constraints, receivable and change-order collection,
acquisition complexity, goodwill, leverage, and a valuation that capitalizes years of strong growth.
The price decline itself does not remove that valuation risk.

Catalysts are backlog conversion with stable or improving margins, free cash flow toward guidance,
broad utility and large-load awards, disciplined acquisitions, and lower leverage. Invalidate the
constructive case if backlog or RPO contracts without conversion, Electric margins deteriorate,
project losses or collection problems rise, free cash flow misses guidance materially, leverage
increases without commensurate earnings, or customer projects are cancelled or deferred.
