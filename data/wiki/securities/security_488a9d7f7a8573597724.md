# Quanta Services, Inc. common stock

## Decision

**Baseline comparison; no conviction strategy.** Quanta is a direct beneficiary of utility-grid,
transmission, substation, interconnection, and large-load construction spending. First-quarter
revenue, earnings, cash flow, remaining performance obligations, and backlog all reached records,
and management raised 2026 guidance. Those strengths are offset by substantial acquisition-related
goodwill and debt, fixed-price and labor execution risk, and an exceptionally demanding valuation.
The July volume anomaly accompanied a 22.06% monthly decline and oversold technical state, but no
contemporaneous primary filing explains the move. It is a material risk and attention signal, not a
trade signal; even a premium bounded base value remains 20.7% below the current mark.

## Business and financial evidence

Quanta provides infrastructure solutions across electric power, transmission, substations,
distribution, interconnections, load centers, communications, underground utilities, pipelines,
and industrial facilities. That makes it a direct construction and skilled-labor channel for
[[ideas/idea_ai_infrastructure_power|AI infrastructure and power bottlenecks]], while demand from
utilities and broader grid modernization is more diversified than the data-center theme alone.

First-quarter 2026 revenue rose 26.3% to USD 7.875 billion and adjusted diluted EPS rose to USD 2.68
from USD 1.78. Electric-segment revenue rose to USD 6.469 billion from USD 4.944 billion and its
operating margin improved to 8.7% from 8.3%. Operating cash flow was USD 391.7 million and free cash
flow was USD 184.4 million. Total remaining performance obligations reached USD 26.2 billion and
total backlog reached USD 48.5 billion, including USD 40.1 billion in Electric backlog.

Backlog quality is not equivalent to guaranteed revenue. Quanta includes estimated MSA renewals and
non-fixed-price work in backlog, and its filing says cancellations, delays, regulatory requirements,
weather, commercial issues, and change-order acceptance can alter timing and realization. Fixed-price
contracts, skilled-labor availability and inflation, customer collection, permitting, supply-chain
constraints, and acquisition integration remain material execution risks.

At March 31, Quanta held USD 364.8 million cash against USD 689.7 million current debt maturities and
USD 5.202 billion long-term debt. Current assets of USD 10.653 billion exceeded current liabilities
of USD 9.384 billion, and the guided USD 1.55-2.05 billion of 2026 free cash flow supports liquidity.
However, goodwill of USD 7.405 billion represented a large part of the USD 25.747 billion asset base,
so acquisition performance, leverage, and impairment risk reduce balance-sheet resilience.

## Thesis, contrary evidence, and invalidation

The constructive thesis is that grid hardening, electrification, generation and transmission
investment, large-load interconnections, and data-center electrical work create a long-duration
project pipeline that Quanta can serve with scarce craft labor and integrated solutions. Record RPO,
backlog, Electric growth, margin improvement, and higher guidance show that demand is converting to
reported earnings and cash.

Contrary evidence is the gap between backlog and firm RPO, dependence on project timing and contract
estimates, fixed-price cost risk, skilled-labor constraints, receivable and change-order collection,
acquisition complexity, goodwill, leverage, and a valuation that capitalizes years of strong growth.
The price decline itself does not remove that valuation risk.

Catalysts are backlog conversion with stable or improving margins, free cash flow toward guidance,
broad utility and large-load awards, disciplined acquisitions, and lower leverage. Invalidate the
constructive case if backlog or RPO contracts without conversion, Electric margins deteriorate,
project losses or collection problems rise, free cash flow misses guidance materially, leverage
increases without commensurate earnings, or customer projects are cancelled or deferred.

## Valuation

The USD 561.14 mark equals **40.4x** the USD 13.90 midpoint of management's 2026 adjusted diluted-EPS
guidance. For a bounded 12-month sensitivity using that disclosed earnings base:

- Downside: **25x** guidance-midpoint EPS, or USD **347.50** per share and **38.1% downside**.
- Base: **32x** guidance-midpoint EPS, or USD **444.80** per share and **20.7% downside**.

This is a scenario range, not a precise price target. The 32x base multiple remains a substantial
premium in recognition of record backlog, double-digit growth, and grid-investment visibility; the
25x case reflects project cyclicality, leverage, acquisition risk, and normalization. Both values
remain below market, so the assessment is useful for baseline comparison but fails the configured
minimum-upside, payoff-ratio, margin-of-safety, and timing gates. No conviction strategy research is
justified.
