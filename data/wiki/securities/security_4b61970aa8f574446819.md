## Business economics

GE Vernova supplies generation equipment and services, wind equipment, and grid-electrification
systems. Its installed fleets create long-duration service revenue, while turbine and grid
equipment depend on capacity, project underwriting, customer schedules, warranties, and execution.

In Q2 2026, orders were USD 24.2 billion, revenue USD 11.1 billion, net income USD 0.6 billion,
adjusted EBITDA USD 1.2 billion, and free cash flow USD 5.1 billion. Backlog reached USD 176 billion.
Gas equipment backlog and slot reservations reached 116 GW, while year-to-date data-centre orders
exceeded USD 5 billion. Management raised 2026 revenue guidance to USD 45.5-46.5 billion and free-
cash-flow guidance to USD 11.5-12.5 billion while retaining 12%-14% adjusted EBITDA-margin guidance.

## Thesis and contrary evidence

GE Vernova is a direct positive-sensitivity exposure to generation and grid bottlenecks. Q2
provides unusually strong confirmation that demand is converting into backlog, margins, and cash.
The filing defines reported backlog as remaining performance obligations and excludes orders that
customers can cancel without substantive penalty, improving its quality as evidence. However, one
working-capital-rich quarter is not normalized free cash flow, non-GAAP margin guidance still
requires delivery, Wind remains an execution counterweight, and capacity and acquisition spending
expose shareholders to cycle and integration risk.

## Valuation

The deterministic 28 July 2026 paper mark is USD 946.00. At 270 million Q2 diluted weighted-average
shares, it implies approximately USD 255.4 billion of equity value. The 2026 free-cash-flow-guidance
midpoint of USD 12.0 billion therefore represents only a 4.70% yield before recognizing that the
quarter and first half benefited materially from working capital.

A bounded 12-month sensitivity, not a precise price target, uses the filing's USD 13.120 billion of
cash and restricted cash less USD 2.794 billion of long-term borrowings and 270 million diluted
shares. The downside case assumes USD 8.0 billion of normalized free cash flow and an 18x multiple,
giving USD 571.58 per share, or **39.6% downside**. The base case assumes USD 11.0 billion and a 24x
multiple, giving USD 1,016.02 per share, or **7.4% upside**. The base case remains below both the
configured 10% minimum base-upside gate and the
configured 20% margin-of-safety requirement; the result is particularly sensitive to working-
capital normalization and the multiple assigned to elevated backlog growth.

## Catalysts and invalidation

Catalysts are profitable backlog conversion, higher service mix, turbine-capacity delivery,
Electrification margin expansion, and data-centre orders becoming revenue. Invalidate the positive
case if cancellations or delays rise, equipment margin deteriorates, Wind losses consume Power and
Electrification gains, capacity expansion overruns, acquisition integration weakens cash returns, or
customer over-ordering causes a backlog correction.
