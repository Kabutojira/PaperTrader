## Business economics

GE Vernova operates across Power, Wind and Electrification. The issuer describes an installed base of approximately 7,000 gas turbines and approximately 59,000 wind turbines and says services represent more than 55% of backlog. These features can support recurring service revenue, customer proximity and visibility beyond initial equipment sales.

That qualitative evidence does not establish current economic quality by itself. A robust assessment still needs segment margins, warranty and project-loss trends, backlog terms, capital intensity, working-capital requirements and consolidated cash conversion. The investor overview is promotional issuer material and is not a substitute for current financial statements.

## Thesis and contrary evidence

The qualitative thesis remains plausible: rising electricity demand and grid constraints can increase demand for generation equipment, long-duration services and electrification systems. A large installed base may create service attachment and replacement opportunities.

The current evidence does not show whether demand is converting into profitable revenue and normalized free cash flow. Material contrary possibilities include backlog delays or cancellations, unfavorable contract economics, Wind losses, warranty exposure, capacity-expansion overruns, customer concentration and working-capital reversal. None is asserted to have occurred; the problem is that accessible current evidence does not quantify or adequately test them.

## Valuation

Method considered: **free-cash-flow yield over a 12-month horizon**.

A supportable calculation requires:

1. normalized annual free cash flow separated from temporary working-capital movements;
2. current cash, restricted cash, borrowings and other claims on enterprise value;
3. diluted shares attributable to this common-stock instrument;
4. evidence-based bear, base and bull multiples;
5. current segment execution and guidance sufficient to assign scenario probabilities; and
6. a verified reference price tied to this instrument after fair values are established.

The accessible sources do not provide those inputs. Bear, base and bull values and probabilities are therefore blank. The supplied market observation is not used for a valuation score because a market price cannot substitute for fair value.
