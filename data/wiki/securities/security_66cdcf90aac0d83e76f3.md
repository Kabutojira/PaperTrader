## Economics and thesis

Albemarle combines lithium conversion and resource interests with Specialties. Q1 2026 sales rose
33% to USD 1.43 billion; Energy Storage sales rose 70% and adjusted EBITDA rose 196% as realized
pricing rose 51%, volumes 14%, and inventory timing and cost actions helped. Operating cash flow was
USD 346 million and company-defined free cash flow USD 248 million. Asset sales funded a USD 1.3
billion debt reduction, leaving USD 1.9 billion total debt and USD 1.1 billion cash.

The recovery is economically meaningful but highly price-sensitive. Management's own scenarios put
2026 adjusted EBITDA at USD 0.9-1.0 billion around USD 10/kg LCE, USD 2.4-2.6 billion around USD
20/kg, and USD 4.2-4.4 billion around USD 30/kg. Forty percent of salts volume is on long-term
agreements, so spot and realized prices can diverge. Mandatory convertible preferred shares also
matter to common-share value.

The 28 July alert is a **strengthening** of that same condition, not an independent fundamental

result establishes a company-specific impairment or recovery sufficient to explain the one-month
decline before that release. The move is best classified as momentum and pre-results timing risk,
with commodity and valuation uncertainty still unresolved.

No supportable common-equity valuation range is recorded. The filing's USD 0.9-1.0 billion to USD
4.2-4.4 billion adjusted-EBITDA span across management's approximately USD 10/kg to USD 30/kg LCE
scenarios is a sensitivity framework, not a probability-weighted mid-cycle forecast. Converting it
to per-share downside and base values would require unsupported lithium-price probabilities,
contract-lag and tax assumptions, terminal multiples, and mandatory-convertible share treatment.
The comparable assessment therefore records `valuation_unsupported`, rather than inventing a price
target.

Catalysts are sustained realized pricing, volume growth, cost savings, positive free cash flow, and
continued leverage reduction. Invalidate if Chinese supply restarts push prices toward the low
case, contract prices lag, inventory timing reverses, capex or commissioning overruns, geopolitical
disruption rises, or dilution absorbs the recovery.
