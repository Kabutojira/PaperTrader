Uber's marketplace economics strengthened in the second quarter of 2026. ## Business economics and evidence

Uber aggregates consumer demand and driver, courier and merchant supply through pricing, dispatch, routing, payments and engagement products. In Q2, monthly active platform consumers reached 208 million. Mobility gross bookings grew 22% and Delivery gross bookings 26%; segment operating income was USD2.215 billion for Mobility and USD1.055 billion for Delivery. Freight reported a USD24 million segment loss, while corporate G&A and platform R&D remained a substantial offset.

Cash conversion is now an important part of the case. Q2 operating cash flow was USD2.862 billion and capital expenditure was USD70 million, producing USD2.792 billion of issuer-defined free cash flow. Six-month free cash flow was USD5.078 billion. Diluted weighted-average shares fell from 2.126 billion to 2.050 billion year over year, although stock-based compensation remained recurring at USD549 million for the quarter.

GAAP net income requires care: Q2 included a USD1.6 billion pre-tax benefit from equity-investment revaluations. Operating income, operating cash flow and free cash flow therefore provide cleaner evidence of recurring progress than headline net income.

## Downside and contrary evidence

Worker-classification and tax disputes have limited precedent and uncertain timing. Insurance reserves increased, and legal or regulatory settlements can consume cash excluded from management's non-GAAP measures. Competitive incentives could weaken margins. Stock compensation remains a recurring economic cost. Autonomous fleets could use Uber's demand and utilization layer, but could instead integrate directly with consumers and weaken the marketplace.

## Catalysts and invalidation

Potential catalysts are sustained activity growth, further operating-margin expansion, trailing free cash flow remaining above USD10 billion, lower diluted shares, improved Delivery economics and evidence that autonomous launches preserve Uber's take rate.

The thesis should be invalidated or materially revised if activity growth stops producing recurring cash-flow growth, regulatory or insurance costs absorb operating leverage, dilution resumes persistently, Mobility or Delivery profitability reverses, or autonomous fleets materially bypass Uber's demand and dispatch network.
