The SEC filing confirms Intuitive Machines, Inc. and its Nasdaq-listed Class A share identity. The
company combines lunar delivery, spacecraft manufacturing, navigation, communications, and mission
operations after acquiring Lanteris Space Systems. No accepted canonical idea-security
relationship currently exists, so this review does not infer one from thematic fit alone.

## Business economics

Intuitive Machines earns product, service, and grant revenue from commercial, civil, and national-
security space programs. Long-duration and fixed-price contracts provide visibility but expose the
company to cost estimates, milestone timing, appropriations, termination for convenience, mission
execution, customer concentration, and launch risk. The January 2026 Lanteris acquisition greatly
expanded spacecraft manufacturing scale while making historical comparisons and integration
execution more difficult.

For the quarter ended 31 March 2026, revenue was USD 186.7 million versus USD 62.5 million a year
earlier, including USD 141.6 million of product revenue. Operating loss was USD 39.2 million and
free cash outflow was USD 64.6 million. Backlog reached USD 1.055 billion, including USD 612.8
million acquired with Lanteris, and management expected to recognize 60-65% during the remainder of
2026. The company maintained full-year revenue guidance of USD 900 million-1 billion and positive
adjusted EBITDA.

## Thesis and contrary evidence

The constructive case is that lunar-delivery heritage, a larger spacecraft platform, navigation
and communications capabilities, and a billion-dollar backlog can establish a scaled space-
infrastructure prime. The contrary case is that much of the step-up was acquired, backlog can be
cancelled or depend on appropriations, fixed-price execution can destroy margins, and growth still
consumes cash.

## Valuation

At USD 12.3599 and 216.8 million reported Class A and Class C shares outstanding at 31 March, the
equity value is approximately USD 2.68 billion. Adding USD 335.8 million of long-term debt and
subtracting USD 231.6 million of cash gives an approximate USD 2.78 billion enterprise value, or
2.93 times the USD 950 million midpoint of 2026 revenue guidance. This is a favorable simplification
because it does not add preferred stock, warrants, employee awards, or other claims and because the
reported share count may change before the 12-month horizon.

A bounded 12-month sensitivity, not a precise price target, uses the reported share count and USD
104.2 million of net debt. A downside case applies 1.5 times the USD 900 million low end of revenue
guidance, producing USD 5.75 per share, or **53.5% downside**. A base case applies 2.5 times the USD
950 million midpoint, producing USD 10.47 per share, or **15.3% downside**. The base multiple still
assumes successful Lanteris integration, backlog conversion, and positive adjusted EBITDA. Neither
case supports the configured margin of safety despite the oversold decline.

## Catalysts and invalidation

Catalysts are August 13 Q2 results, organic growth disclosure, backlog conversion, positive cash
generation, successful Lanteris integration, further funded awards, and mission execution. Invalidate
the constructive case if backlog is cancelled or fails to convert, fixed-price programs produce
material losses, liquidity erodes without operating progress, integration disrupts controls or
delivery, key missions fail, or dilution overwhelms per-share growth.

mission, financing, or guidance news. No paper strategy or signal is justified.
