## Business economics

Intuitive Machines is assembling a broader space-infrastructure platform spanning spacecraft manufacturing, lunar delivery, communications networks, mission operations, and ground infrastructure. A broader offering could increase customer relevance, create recurring service opportunities, and improve utilization if awarded contracts convert on acceptable margins.

Current economics remain investment-heavy. The issuer produced substantial revenue but remained loss-making and consumed USD 145.819 million of free cash flow in the first half. Product revenue carried better direct economics than service revenue in Q2, but consolidated operating expenses, depreciation, research, administration, acquisition costs, and working-capital requirements kept results negative. Positive adjusted EBITDA guidance is therefore an intermediate operating target, not evidence of GAAP profitability or positive free cash flow.

## Backlog quality and contrary evidence

Reported backlog is an indicator of potential sales, not guaranteed revenue or cash. The issuer defines it as estimated future revenue from awarded contracts less amounts already recognized. Its reconciliation shows that USD 612.8 million of June backlog came from the January Lanteris acquisition. It also included more than USD 600 million of estimated program value for three commercial satellites after the company had received USD 45 million of authority to proceed. Backlog was reduced by performance and by USD 15.5 million of adjustments, principally a rideshare-contract descoping.

This composition matters. Headline backlog does not establish funding certainty, timing, margin, collectability, or the amount of presently authorized work. The constructive case depends on converting awards into funded execution and cash rather than merely increasing estimated program values.

## Thesis

The constructive thesis is that quarterly revenue above USD 200 million, a diversified USD 1.762 billion backlog, and USD 900 million to USD 1 billion of annual guidance demonstrate meaningful aerospace scale. Commercial GEO satellites, national-security spacecraft, lunar programs, and acquired communications infrastructure could reduce dependence on any single lunar mission.

The contrary evidence is material: Q2 remained loss-making, first-half free cash flow was deeply negative, and the capital structure includes debt, warrants, preferred claims, Class C interests, and substantial redeemable noncontrolling interests. The August Goonhilly and COMSAT acquisition closed after the June balance-sheet date, so June cash and debt are not a current post-closing capitalization snapshot.

## Valuation method

A forward revenue multiple is the clearest provisional framework for a rapidly scaling company without durable earnings. The required arithmetic is:

`fair value per share = (forward revenue × selected EV/revenue multiple + cash − debt − other senior economic claims) ÷ normalized fully diluted Class A-equivalent claims`

The release now supplies much of the June financial picture, but it still does not support that complete equation. In particular:

1. **The balance sheet is pre-acquisition.** June cash and debt precede the August Goonhilly and COMSAT closing.
2. **The denominator is incomplete.** The 162.172 million figure is a quarterly weighted-average Class A count, not period-end fully diluted economic claims.
3. **Other claims require contractual interpretation.** Debt, warrant liabilities, preferred stock, Class C interests, LLC units, and redeemable noncontrolling interests cannot all be added to or deducted from equity value mechanically without their exchange and settlement terms.
4. **Acquired economics remain unquantified.** The release does not provide enough Goonhilly and COMSAT revenue, margin, integration-cost, capital-requirement, or purchase-price information.
5. **No supportable multiple range was verified.** Applying an arbitrary revenue multiple or reverse-engineering one from the market price would not constitute independent fair value.

## Scenario framework

- **Bear:** Begin with the USD 900 million guidance floor and assume weak conversion, contract descoping, fixed-price pressure, continued negative free cash flow, acquisition costs, and a conservative multiple.
- **Base:** Begin near the guidance midpoint and assume adequate execution, funded program progression, and gradual margin improvement.
- **Bull:** Require revenue near or above USD 1 billion, stronger funded-award conversion, improving margins and cash flow, successful missions, and profitable communications expansion.

No fair values or probabilities are assigned. The missing post-closing capitalization, diluted denominator, acquired economics, and multiple evidence affect every scenario and cannot be cured by using the October market price as a proxy.

## Downside

Downside can arise from delayed appropriations or customer authority, cancellation or descoping, fixed-price overruns, launch or mission failure, supplier constraints, customer concentration, integration problems, working-capital needs, and financing dilution. The backlog reconciliation demonstrates that estimated total program value may materially exceed presently disclosed authority to proceed. The negative first-half free cash flow and layered capital structure also mean that the June cash balance alone does not establish liquidity resilience or Class A equity value.

## Catalysts

Potential catalysts include:

- conversion of backlog into revenue and operating cash flow;
- delivery within the USD 900 million to USD 1 billion outlook;
- positive adjusted EBITDA accompanied by better free cash flow;
- additional funded authority on major programs;
- successful lunar and national-security mission execution;
- quantified revenue, margin, and cash contribution from Goonhilly and COMSAT; and
- a subsequent filing that reconciles post-closing cash, debt, acquisition accounting, diluted claims, integration costs, and cash flow.

## Invalidation

Invalidate the constructive operating thesis if revenue materially misses guidance, major awards fail to receive funding authority, acquired backlog converts poorly, fixed-price losses persist, adjusted EBITDA improvement does not lead toward cash generation, integration disrupts execution or controls, important missions fail, or dilution and senior claims grow faster than enterprise value. Withdraw any future valuation when its net-debt or diluted-claim reconciliation cannot be reproduced from primary evidence.
