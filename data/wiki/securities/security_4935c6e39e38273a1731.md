# MercadoLibre, Inc. common stock

## Research conclusion

MercadoLibre has a strong but investment-intensive commerce and fintech ecosystem. The updated 12-month valuation is **USD 1,440 / USD 2,200 / USD 3,120** per share for bear, base and bull cases, weighted **30% / 50% / 20%**. The resulting probability-weighted value is **USD 2,156**. Confidence is **medium** because the latest accessible primary evidence is an unaudited issuer release through June 30, 2026, and the forward earnings and valuation multiples are analyst assumptions rather than company guidance.

## Business economics and thesis

MercadoLibre combines marketplace demand, logistics, payments, financial accounts, acquiring, advertising and credit. The economic thesis is that commerce supplies users and merchants to Mercado Pago, while payments, credit and loyalty increase purchasing frequency and retention. In Q2 2026, ecosystemic users generated materially more commerce and fintech activity than single-product users, supporting the cross-sell mechanism.

The scale evidence remains strong. Q2 net revenue and financial income increased 50% year over year to USD 10.169 billion. FX-neutral GMV grew 36% to USD 21.9 billion, TPV grew 56% to approximately USD 101 billion, fintech monthly active users reached 88 million and quarterly unique active buyers reached 89 million. MELI+ subscribers grew 72%, while Brazil’s lower free-shipping threshold was associated with higher conversion and purchase frequency.

The contrary evidence is equally important. Q2 operating income declined 17% to USD 683 million, operating margin compressed 550 basis points to 6.7%, and net income declined 11% to USD 466 million. Management attributed the pressure to marketplace pricing initiatives, shipping costs, acquiring investment and credit growth, but the eventual return on those investments remains unverified.

## Credit, funding and cash flow

The credit portfolio exceeded USD 16 billion and grew 75% year over year. Consolidated NIMAL improved sequentially but declined year over year to 20.7%. Credit-card NIMAL was negative 2.5%, although the issuer reported a 4.6% 15-90-day credit-card NPL ratio and a 7.0% ratio for the overall portfolio, both near historical lows. Management expects newly issued card cohorts to become more profitable as they mature; the valuation treats that as an assumption, not an established outcome.

Provision for doubtful accounts was USD 1.276 billion in Q2, up from USD 690 million a year earlier. First-half operating cash flow was USD 5.737 billion, but it included growth in customer funds and other fintech working-capital balances. Q2 adjusted free cash flow was USD 214 million after USD 441 million of capital expenditure and USD 2.1 billion invested in credit expansion. Reported net debt rose to USD 6.425 billion from USD 4.682 billion at December 31, 2025.

## Valuation method and arithmetic

A forward earnings multiple is used because a conventional consolidated free-cash-flow multiple is difficult to interpret for a business containing deposits, payment settlement balances, receivables and credit funding. Reported first-half diluted EPS was USD 17.42 and Q2 diluted EPS was USD 9.19.

- **Bear — USD 1,440, 30% probability:** USD 36 next-12-month diluted EPS multiplied by 40x. Growth continues, but credit provisions, funding costs, incentives and logistics spending prevent meaningful margin recovery.
- **Base — USD 2,200, 50% probability:** USD 44 EPS multiplied by 50x. Commerce and fintech retain strong local-currency growth, credit remains controlled and operating margin recovers gradually.
- **Bull — USD 3,120, 20% probability:** USD 52 EPS multiplied by 60x. Engagement, advertising, payments and credit cross-sell outperform while maturing cohorts and logistics density produce substantial operating leverage.

The weighted calculation is `(USD 1,440 × 30%) + (USD 2,200 × 50%) + (USD 3,120 × 20%) = USD 2,156`. The lower bull probability recognizes that both strong earnings delivery and retention of a very high multiple are required. These values are independently modeled fair-value scenarios and do not use the market price as a proxy.

## Downside

The central downside is that incentives and investment prove structurally necessary rather than temporary. Operating margin could remain depressed if free shipping, seller discounts, acquiring-device costs and user acquisition continue to absorb scale benefits. Rapid credit growth can magnify small underwriting errors, while higher losses or funding costs could reduce both earnings and confidence in the ecosystem. Currency volatility, weak consumption, competition and regulation across multiple Latin American jurisdictions add further risk. Lower earnings and multiple compression could occur together.

## Catalysts

Potential catalysts are continued growth in ecosystemic users, purchase frequency and category breadth; recovery in credit-card NIMAL as cohorts mature; stable delinquency despite portfolio growth; better shipping unit economics; advertising and MELI+ expansion; AI-enabled productivity; and evidence that operating margins recover as current investments scale. Subsequent quarterly results should test whether revenue growth is converting into earnings and discretionary cash generation.

## Invalidation conditions

The thesis would be invalidated by sustained deterioration in delinquency, credit losses or funding access; credit-card NIMAL remaining materially negative after cohort maturation; persistent margin compression despite greater scale; weak discretionary cash generation; declining commerce or payments engagement; or regulation that materially impairs payments, deposits, credit or logistics. Evidence that subsidies must continually rise merely to maintain growth would also invalidate the base case.

## Limitations

The EPS forecasts, multiples and probabilities are assumptions rather than issuer guidance, and no separate valuation was completed for the commerce, payments and credit businesses.
