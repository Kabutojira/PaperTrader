## Economics and thesis

SQM produces lithium brine products through the Chilean Nova Andino Litio partnership with Codelco,
plus Australian hard-rock and conversion assets, iodine, and specialty plant nutrition. Q2 2026
revenue was USD 2.47 billion and net income was USD 660.0 million or USD 2.31 per share. Lithium
volume reached a record 84.1 thousand tonnes LCE, the Novandino realized lithium price rose to about
USD 21.8/kg, and lithium supplied 78% of first-half consolidated gross profit.

The brine asset offers scale and cost potential, but state contributions are economically material:
Nova Andino paid more than USD 530 million to the Chilean state in the quarter. The partnership,
royalty and tax structure, environmental approvals for Salar Futuro, water use, international ramp,
and contract-price transmission must be valued explicitly. At the end of June, long-term debt was
USD 4.79 billion, short-term debt was USD 0.43 billion, and cash was USD 3.38 billion. The USD 3
billion 2026-2028 capex plan and rising receivables and inventory limit how much reported earnings
can be treated as distributable owner cash.

## Current alert and new primary evidence

The canonical alert period ran from 22 July through 19 August 2026. The ADS rose 26.42% over that
period to USD 81.96, and 19 August volume reached 2.88 million shares with a 2.51 z-score. The move
followed the 18 August Q2 filing and is therefore consistent with a fundamental repricing rather
than unexplained volume noise. The filing disclosed much stronger sequential lithium pricing,
record volume and earnings, but the market move also reduces valuation asymmetry. This is positive
operating evidence and a timing risk, not a validated paper-trade opportunity.

The Q2 release also disclosed that Salar Futuro's environmental and technical documentation was
submitted in July. Subject to approvals, the project contemplates about USD 3 billion of investment
over seven years and seeks higher recovery with no continental-water use in the production process.
Separately, Mt Holland ran at full capacity; SQM's share of the approved expansion is expected to
cost USD 450-500 million through 2029 and target first production in 2030. These milestones add
duration and diversification while increasing permitting, execution and capital-allocation risk.
