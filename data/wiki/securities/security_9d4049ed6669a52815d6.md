## Business economics

SQM has scaled exposure to lithium through the Chilean Nova Andino Litio partnership and the Australian Covalent Lithium joint venture, alongside iodine and specialty plant nutrition. Lithium economics are driven by realized prices, tonnes sold, operating costs, contractual and tax payments to the Chilean state, partnership attribution, and the capital required to maintain and expand production. Iodine and plant nutrition diversify gross profit but do not eliminate lithium-cycle sensitivity.

The issuer-authored [first-half 2026 results release](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/sqm-reports-earnings-for-the-six-months-ended-june-30-2026-ce7859ddd088f220) (`source_web_90359067163d38116ae750c7`) reported:

- First-half revenue of USD 4.23 billion and net income of USD 1.02 billion, or USD 3.59 per share.
- Second-quarter net income of USD 660.0 million, or USD 2.31 per share.
- Record quarterly lithium volume above 84 thousand tonnes of lithium-carbonate equivalent.
- More than USD 1.6 billion accrued in first-half payments to the Chilean state, including taxes, contractual payments and the dividend accrued for Codelco.
- Lithium representing 78% of first-half consolidated gross profit.

These facts verify strong operating leverage to the lithium recovery, but they also show why reported earnings should not be extrapolated mechanically. The earnings base is commodity-sensitive, lithium concentration is high, and state participation is economically material.

## Expansion evidence

The [SGX announcement page](https://links.sgx.com/1.0.0/corporate-announcements/HNQGSKX1PAXMY9YZ/ac956cf9b60b1aa8da12b346ac29bca8c25d7aa2e11721001ffdaf0c4393d08f) (`source_sqm_mt_holland_fid_2026`) authenticates Wesfarmers' Mt Holland final-investment-decision disclosure. The attached [Wesfarmers release](https://links.sgx.com/FileOpen/WES26-016%20Mt%20Holland%20lithium%20expansion%20project%20-%20final%20investment%20decision.ashx?App=Announcement&FileID=896975) (`source_web_3df355848bedc5a9e78d6acc`) says the jointly approved expansion is designed to increase 100%-basis spodumene concentrate nameplate capacity from approximately 380,000 to 760,000 tonnes annually. Construction of the second concentrator is expected to begin in the second half of 2027, with initial expansion production expected in the first half of 2030. Wesfarmers estimated its own share of project capital expenditure at USD 450-500 million, excluding life-of-mine capital and capitalized interest.

The expansion could improve scale and unit costs, but it is not a near-term earnings catalyst. Its long schedule, remaining approvals, construction risk and funding needs support a conservative treatment within a 12-month valuation.

## Thesis

SQM offers material upside if battery and stationary-storage demand absorb new lithium supply and sustain attractive realized prices. First-half 2026 results demonstrate that the operating leverage is real. The counterweight is that lithium supplied most consolidated gross profit, state payments are substantial, and multiple projects require capital before their economics are fully demonstrated. The appropriate valuation therefore normalizes earnings rather than annualizing the unusually strong second quarter.

## Downside and contrary evidence

The strongest operating evidence is also the principal contrary evidence to an unqualified bullish thesis: lithium generated 78% of first-half gross profit. A renewed surplus could reduce realized prices faster than operating costs, taxes, state payments and committed investment decline. Other downside paths include unfavorable Nova Andino attribution, increased state take, delayed environmental approvals, weak Australian conversion performance, Mt Holland cost overruns, higher debt, and iodine-price normalization.

The Mt Holland decision confirms long-term supply growth rather than scarcity alone. Its planned doubling of concentrate capacity is strategically positive for SQM but also illustrates how incumbent and new supply can respond to favorable lithium economics.

## Catalysts

- Sustained lithium volume and realized pricing across multiple reporting periods.
- Evidence that accounting earnings convert into cash after taxes, state payments, working capital and capital expenditure.
- Progress on Salar Futuro approvals and clearer attributable economics.
- Improved performance at Australian conversion assets.
- On-budget Mt Holland milestones, although first expansion output is not expected until 2030.
- Continued resilience in iodine earnings.

## Invalidation conditions

Invalidate the USD 77 base case if normalized EPS can no longer reasonably reach USD 5.50 because lithium prices or volumes deteriorate persistently, Nova Andino allocates materially less economics to SQM shareholders, state take rises beyond the modeled burden, Australian assets fail to approach commercial performance, or debt and capital expenditure grow without commensurate attributable cash flow.

Reassess the base case upward only if several reporting periods demonstrate sustainable owner cash generation consistent with at least USD 7.50 normalized EPS and major projects advance without material cost overruns, dilution or leverage. A temporary commodity-price spike by itself is insufficient.

## Lithium-cycle relationship

SQM remains a positive, medium-confidence beneficiary of [[ideas/idea_critical_minerals_lithium|Critical minerals and the lithium cycle]]. Battery and stationary-storage demand can transmit through sales volume, realized lithium prices and utilization of SQM's asset base. Record quarterly volume and lithium's gross-profit concentration verify that mechanism. The relationship would weaken if rapid supply growth, substitution, contract lags, state participation, execution problems or capital intensity prevent higher commodity demand from becoming attributable shareholder cash flow.
